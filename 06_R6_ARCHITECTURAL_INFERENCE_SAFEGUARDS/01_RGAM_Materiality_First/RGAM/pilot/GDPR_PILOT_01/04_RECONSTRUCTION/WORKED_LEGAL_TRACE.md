## Trace 1 — SC-10 "Remedies and judicial review" (seeded draw)

### Chain: provision → statement → record → S → A

| Step | Provisions | Institutional statement (extracted) | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Art. 77(1) | every data subject "shall have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority" | QR-0204 (SA→data subject; entitlement of the counterpart; review/adjudication) |
| 2 | Arts 57(1)(f), 77(2), 60(7)–(8) | SA must handle and investigate "to the extent appropriate", inform the complainant of "progress and outcome … including the possibility of a judicial remedy pursuant to Article 78"; the SA with which the complaint was lodged informs the complainant or, if dismissed, decides and notifies | QR-0120, QR-0205, QR-0143, QR-0144 |
| 3 | Art. 12(4) | controller that does not act on a request must inform the data subject of "the possibility of lodging a complaint with a supervisory authority and seeking a judicial remedy" | QR-0008 (interface to SC-01) |
| 4 | Art. 78(1)–(3) | courts of the Member State where the SA is established: effective remedy against "a legally binding decision of a supervisory authority"; and where the competent SA "does not handle a complaint or does not inform the data subject within three months on the progress or outcome of the complaint lodged pursuant to Article 77" | QR-0206, QR-0207, NA-0126 |
| 5 | Art. 78(4) | SA must forward the Board opinion/decision that preceded the challenged decision to the court | QR-0208 (dependency on the consistency mechanism) |
| 6 | Arts 79(1)–(2), 82(1)–(6) | remedy against controller/processor "without prejudice to … the right to lodge a complaint … pursuant to Article 77"; forum by establishment or residence; compensation claims in the same forum | QR-0209, 0215–0218, NA-0127–0129 |
| 7 | Art. 80(1)–(2) | mandate of a qualifying body for 77–79 (and 82 where national law provides); optional independent action by national option | QR-0210, QR-0211 |
| 8 | Arts 58(5), 83(9), 81 | SA power to refer/litigate (to be conferred by national law); fine initiation before national courts; court-to-court coordination | NA-0105, QR-0130, QR-0225, QR-0212–0214 |

### I1–I4

- **I1 problem and scale.** *Which legally constituted routes let an individual contest conduct of controllers/processors and decisions or inaction of supervisory authorities, and how are they related?* Union instrument acting through Member State authorities and courts; baseline interval from 2018-05-25.
- **I2 qualified coupling (internal, A=S simple case).** (a) **Conditional dependency**: the 78(2) remedy exists only where a complaint under Art. 77 was lodged and the SA did not act or inform within three months. (b) **Information interface**: 77(2) and 12(4) oblige SA/controller to tell the individual about the court route. (c) **Cumulative routes**: 78(1) and 79(1) are stated "without prejudice to" the administrative route. (d) **Mandate** (80(1)) spans the routes. (e) **Forum rule** 82(6)→79(2). (f) **Mandatory interface** 78(4) to the consistency mechanism.
- **I3 configurational consequence.** A *multi-route redress structure*: parallel routes, a conditional escalation from SA inaction to a court and decentralised forums, with a single Union-level antecedent passed to the court. Bearer: S. The property arises from relations between relations (conditional and "without prejudice" clauses); counting remedies would not show it.
- **I4 boundary and defeaters.** Full-instrument screening was done (389 units). Within W there is no provision granting an individual a direct remedy against the Board — **an absence-dependent claim valid for W only** (Treaty-based review is outside W). The content of "effective judicial remedy", the procedure and the court structure depend on Member State law (EXT-015 and generic national-law dependencies). Rival groupings: complaint handling and fines inside SC-02; Art. 82 as private enforcement.

### Anti-circularity answers (directive §6.4)

1. *Which provisions independently support the coupling?* 78(2), 77(2), 12(4), 79(1), 80(1), 82(6), 78(4), 58(5), 83(9).
2. *Was integration assumed before examining the relationships?* No. The S was drawn from a text pattern and **curated by inspection**; the Chapter VIII title was not used (Arts 83–84, penalties, are not part of the S).
3. *Configurational rather than compositional?* The sequence and cumulation exist between relations.
4. *Rule linking evidence to claim?* V6 §6.7 step 3 + I2/I3 (v0.6.1 §6.2).
5. *Could a different grouping explain the same evidence?* Yes: complaint handling as part of supervision (SC-02). Both readings fit; the overlap is recorded.
6. *Defensible under another boundary?* The existence of the routes is stable; their content is not.

### Outcome (AI-01)
**Integrated architecture (simple case A=S) for the redress routes as written in W; overlapping with SC-02 and SC-07; content-level claims indeterminate (external dependency).** Confidence: medium. Human adjudication needed on (i) whether cumulation is a configurational property, (ii) the treatment of national procedural law.

## Trace 2 — SC-07 "Cooperation and consistency" (largest supporting base)

### Chain

| Step | Provisions | Statement | Records |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Art. 56(1) | the SA of the main/single establishment "shall be competent to act as lead supervisory authority for the cross-border processing … in accordance with the procedure provided in Article 60" | QR-0111 |
| 2 | Art. 56(2)–(5) | local competence for local cases; local SA informs the lead; the lead decides within three weeks whether to handle; else Arts 61–62 | QR-0112–0115 |
| 3 | Art. 60(1)–(6) | cooperation; draft decision to concerned SAs; objection within four weeks; revised draft; deemed agreement | QR-0133–0140 |
| 4 | Art. 60(4) → Art. 63 → Art. 65(1)(a) | if the lead "does not follow the relevant and reasoned objection" the matter goes to the consistency mechanism; the Board "shall adopt a binding decision" | QR-0138, QR-0171 |
| 5 | Art. 65(2),(4),(6) | binding decision, standstill, and the lead's final decision "on the basis of" it, attaching it | QR-0173, 0174, 0176 |
| 6 | Art. 64(1)–(8) | six categories of SA measures need a Board opinion; standstill 64(6); the SA takes "utmost account", reports within two weeks whether it maintains the draft, and gives grounds if it does not follow (64(8) → 65(1)(c)) | QR-0162–0170 |
| 7 | Arts 61(8), 62(7), 66 | refusal/silence in assistance or joint operations permits provisional measures and presumes urgency → urgent Board opinion/decision; derogating urgency procedure | QR-0154, 0161, 0178–0181 |
| 8 | Arts 51(2)–(3), 68(3) | duty to cooperate; Board composed of heads of national SAs | QR-0105, QR-0182 |

### I1–I4

- **I1.** *How is application of the Regulation kept consistent where several national authorities are competent?* Union-wide network + Union body; baseline interval.
- **I2 (A=S).** **Qualified procedural dependencies** along 56→60→63→65; **conditional dependency** on the cross-border criterion (56(1)/56(2)); **integration through legally distributed decisions**: the Board's decision binds the lead authority and the SAs concerned (65(2)) and the final decision is bound by it (65(6)).
- **I3.** Authority is distributed among national authorities at first instance and concentrated in the Board at the dispute stage; an urgency path allows unilateral provisional measures with ex-post Board involvement. Bearer: S; and a positional property of the Board — final decision-maker among SAs within W (absence-dependent, W only).
- **I4.** Coverage full; the SAs themselves are **N2-referenced** (Arts 51(1), 54(1)(a)) and constituted by national law outside W; the EDPS (a Board member) is constituted elsewhere (EXT-017); reviewability of Board decisions is outside W. Rival groupings: split 60–62 / 63–67; Board's soft-law tasks (70(1)) as a separate S.

### Anti-circularity answers
1. *Independent provisions:* 56(1), 56(3), 60(4), 64(1), 64(6)–(8), 65(1)–(2), 65(6), 61(8), 62(7), 66.
2. *Integration assumed?* No — the "consistency" label is the Regulation's own; coupling comes from conditional cross-references.
3. *Configurational?* The escalation path exists only through linkage of relations.
4. *Rule:* v0.6.1 §6.2 and V6 §5.2.1 per stage; no score.
5. *Alternative grouping?* Yes (two S coupled by 60(4)→65).
6. *Boundary sensitivity:* with national law inside the corpus, SA instances and powers could differ; the type-level coupling stays; with Treaty remedies inside, the "final decision-maker" claim weakens.

### Outcome (AI-02)
**Integrated architecture (simple case A=S) at type level; peripheral Board tasks distinguishable; claims that depend on the national constitution of SAs or on Treaty review indeterminate.** Confidence: medium-high.

### Reachability is not transmission
No claim above relies on a graph path. For example, that a concerned SA is connected to the Board via the lead authority is **not** read as a transmission of its objection; the transmission exists only because 60(4) and 65(1)(a) say so.
