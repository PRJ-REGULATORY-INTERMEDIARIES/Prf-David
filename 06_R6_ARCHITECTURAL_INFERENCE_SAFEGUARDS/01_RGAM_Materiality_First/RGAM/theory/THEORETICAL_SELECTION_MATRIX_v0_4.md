## 1. Selection criteria derived from David's problem

David's V6 asks how law constitutes a governance architecture and how that architecture can be reconstructed, traceably, from legal text. Reading V6 for what any supporting theory must be able to do yields nine criteria. Each cites the V6 passage that generates it.

| ID | Criterion | Why V6 requires it |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Configurational primacy** — properties arise from arrangement of relations, not the inventory of parts | "Architecture is an emergent property of a configuration" (V6 §5.2) |
| C2 | **Part/whole property separation** — can say which properties belong to a part, a relation, a subconfiguration or the whole | atomistic and ecological fallacies (V6 §6.8) |
| C3 | **Qualified, typed relations** — edges that are not observationally equivalent | "relationships are typed rather than generic" (V6 §6.3) |
| C4 | **Traceability to inscription** — each claim returns to text/provision | vertical traceability (V6 §6.9) |
| C5 | **Stratified ontology** — formal vs implemented vs operational; existence ≠ observability | V6 §1, §6.13; implementation/functional fallacies |
| C6 | **Plural, overlapping architectures** — no forced single label | V6 §5.2, §5.2.3 |
| C7 | **Explicit boundary and scale** — corpus/regime/time delimited | container fallacy; regime boundary (V6 §6.7) |
| C8 | **Non-displacement** — does not turn the project into implementation studies nor require every V6 category to be coded | David: map ≠ universal codebook; V6 §6.13 |
| C9 | **Honest transfer** — if borrowed from another field, states what does and does not transfer | avoid analogy-by-label (v0.3 §2) |

## 4. Why Hillier & Hanson add value *beyond* an inventory of David's categories

Stated as a conditional claim, not a discovery.

1. **V6 asserts emergence; H&H provide a way of treating it.** V6 says architecture is emergent and not inferable from one actor or instrument. The configurational tradition makes this operational: the properties of a *position* are defined by its relations to *all* others (a part's configurational property is determined by the whole). That yields a distinct class of **positional properties** (e.g., being a necessary stage; depth from the rule-maker) that cannot be read off a part's own text.
2. **The same-inventory / different-configuration test.** Two arrangements with identical elements but different relations can differ in properties. This gives V6's qualitative claim ("two laws … similar … different architectures") a *falsifiable contrast design*: hold the position-and-function inventory constant, vary the relations, and check whether the claimed architectural property changes.
3. **Local arrangement vs global structure** maps onto S vs A and disciplines when a local finding may be generalized (container and ecological fallacies).
4. **Boundary and permeability.** The tradition treats thresholds between spaces as analytic objects. RGAM's analogue is the **interface** (e.g., a register or accreditation step through which governance passes between public and private positions). V6 has public-private allocation and autonomy dimensions but not an explicit interface class.
5. **Epistemic move.** *Ideas Are in Things* (Hillier, Hanson & Graham 1987) concerns inferring social principles from observable configuration of artefacts. This is an *epistemological parallel* to V6's route from legal text to governance logic — **not** an equivalence of space syntax and leximetrics.

**What H&H do not add:** no theory of regulation or of legal meaning; their graph is justified by movement and co-presence, whereas legal relations are normatively constituted. **What does not transfer:** space-syntax measures (integration, choice, etc.) presuppose a well-defined adjacency and a metric of depth; legal relations have neither by default. RGAM therefore transfers the **analytic structure** (part/whole property bearers, contrast design, boundary thinking), and treats any computed measure as a *later, separately justified* step. `[CITATION CHECK]` for every H&H attribution above.

**The claim fails if** the same-inventory contrast cannot be instantiated in real legal text (to be tested in the Phase 3 demonstration), or if David's ten dimensions already capture items 1–4 without remainder.
