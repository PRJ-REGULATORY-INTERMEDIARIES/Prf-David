# Architectural theory and method: a proposed extension

This document separates architectural scholarship from V8's authorial propositions. The project draws selectively on three methodological ideas: Alexander's warning against treating complex organization as a single exclusive hierarchy; Hillier and Hanson's attention to configuration, position and part–whole relations; and architecture-description principles that distinguish an object from a representation of it. No spatial metric is transferred mechanically to legal materials.

## Eight proposed operations

1. **Delimitation:** declare problem, documentary universe, domain, geographical scale, period and boundary rule.
2. **Decomposition:** identify legal units and institutional statements while retaining normative context.
3. **Relational reconstruction:** identify qualified relations only when parties and a governance-relevant connection are evidenced.
4. **Configurational reconstruction:** group situated combinations of elements and relations; overlap and multiple membership remain possible.
5. **Positional analysis:** test whether an element's significance derives from its place in an arrangement—e.g., a legally necessary gateway—not simply its prevalence.
6. **Part–whole analysis:** distinguish intrinsic, relational, positional and whole-level properties; state the bearer before generalizing.
7. **Architectural inference:** state the scope, coupling, consequence, alternatives and uncertainty behind a higher-order claim.
8. **Boundary and sensitivity analysis:** ask whether credible alternative boundaries, constituent choices or relationship interpretations change the result. Removal is an optional sensitivity device, never a universal criterion.

## Distinctive methodological question

Architectural configuration theory contributes a testable question that counts cannot answer: **given a comparable inventory of actors, functions, mechanisms and instruments, does a different legally qualified arrangement change the location, sequence, dependency, substitutability or consequence of governance capacity?** If no such difference is demonstrated, the added architectural layer has not earned its complexity.

This is an additional methodological hypothesis. It does not claim that V8 lacks configurational reasoning, nor that a visual model demonstrates a legal architecture.

## Test of theoretical innovation

The architectural contribution is warranted only if it changes a reconstruction using the **same legal evidence**. Its proposed tests are: whether decomposition separates a normative assignment from a relationship that a graph would otherwise fabricate; whether interfaces and cross-provision dependencies identify a legally consequential connection; whether situated position distinguishes a mandatory gateway from a frequent peripheral actor; whether part–whole analysis prevents an element-level observation from being reported as a whole-level property; and whether alternative boundaries change the conclusion. A diagram that displays V8 categories without improving one of these operations is presentation, not innovation.
