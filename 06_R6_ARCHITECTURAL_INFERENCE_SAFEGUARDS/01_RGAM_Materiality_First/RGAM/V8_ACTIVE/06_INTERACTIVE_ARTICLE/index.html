Proposed architectural method

Eight operations, separate from direct coding

Delimitation. State governance problem, documentary universe, domain, geography, period and boundary. A document boundary is not automatically an architecture boundary.
Decomposition. Extract legal units and institutional statements without losing modality or context.
Relational reconstruction. Reconstruct qualified relations only where parties and governance-relevant connection are evidenced.
Configurational reconstruction. Identify situated combinations; overlap and multiple membership remain possible.
Positional analysis. Ask whether an actor, device or relation matters because of its location, sequence or dependency.
Part–whole analysis. Identify whether a property belongs to a constituent, relationship, situated position or whole arrangement.
Architectural inference. Record scope, coupling, consequence, alternatives and uncertainty before making a higher-order claim.
Boundary/sensitivity analysis. Check credible alternative boundaries or readings. Removal is an optional sensitivity procedure, not a universal test.

Exploratory legal illustration

GDPR: cooperation and consistency

Declared source: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, CELEX 32016R0679, historical EUR-Lex/OJ baseline with 2018 corrigendum applied. The source parser materialized 99 articles, 389 paragraph units and 382 point units. All parsed operative units received machine-first screening; this is not complete expert interpretation.

1. Allocation: Article 56 makes the lead supervisory authority competent for qualifying cross-border processing and requires a local authority to inform it in stated cases.
2. Cooperation: Article 60 organizes exchange, draft circulation and reasoned objections among lead and concerned authorities.
3. Conditional escalation: Article 60(4) refers an unresolved qualifying objection to the consistency mechanism in Article 63.
4. Binding dispute stage: Article 65 gives the Board a binding decision in listed disputes, addressed to and binding on the lead and concerned authorities.
5. Final decision: Article 65(6) requires the lead authority or relevant complaint authority to adopt the final decision on the basis of the Board decision. The Board's binding decision is therefore distinguished from the final decisional act toward the controller or processor.

Preliminary inference: within this limited cross-border scope, the provisions support a conditionally integrated cooperation-and-consistency configuration: authority is distributed at first instance and concentrated at the qualifying dispute stage. Opinion/standstill and urgency routes qualify the ordinary sequence. No whole-GDPR architecture, operational result, complete accountability arrangement or independent validation is claimed.
