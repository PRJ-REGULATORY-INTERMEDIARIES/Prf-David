# From Legal Text to Governance Architecture: A Morphological–Relational Method for Reconstructing Regulatory Governance

**Working draft for scientific discussion.** This is a preliminary conceptual article, not a completed, accepted or independently validated paper. David Levi-Faur's V8 manuscript is the authorial baseline. The operational method proposed here is separate from V8's own propositions.

## 1. Introduction: why governance architecture requires reconstruction

Law does not merely set substantive standards. It distributes governing work, capacity, judgment and answerability. Yet none of those features is automatically disclosed by an inventory of provisions or actors. The methodological problem is reconstructive: how can legal text support a defensible claim about the organization of governance without mistaking a document, a graph, or a frequently occurring term for an architecture?

V8 supplies the central answer: relational leximetrics moves from legal text and institutional statements to relationships, configurations, architectures and regimes through explicit inference. This article asks whether a morphological–relational method can make that movement more inspectable by distinguishing components, relations, positions in a configuration and whole-level properties.

## 2. Architecture as a disciplinary and theoretical concept

Architecture is useful here only in its configurational sense. The relevant issue is not visual design but organized relations and their consequences. A configuration may have properties that no individual constituent possesses: a role can be a necessary stage, an authority can be central only in relation to alternatives, and a sequence can distribute decision capacity across multiple bodies. A legal reconstruction must nevertheless avoid treating a spatial analogy as legal proof.

## 3. Configurational thinking: components, relations, position and whole

Alexander's critique of exclusive tree-like organization motivates caution about forcing all arrangements into non-overlapping categories. Hillier and Hanson motivate attention to relational position and part–whole effects. These are selective inspirations. They do not provide legal metrics or a ready-made ontology.

The proposed distinction is practical: an intrinsic property is conferred on a constituent; a relational property qualifies a connection; a positional property depends on a constituent's place in an arrangement; and a whole-level property concerns the arrangement. The analyst should state which bearer is claimed before generalizing.

## 4. The conceptual structure of regulatory governance in V8

V8 separates observable objects, actors, functions, mechanisms, instruments and relationships from authority, discretion and accountability. Functions identify governance work; mechanisms identify how influence operates; instruments are concrete institutional devices; and relationships identify how actors are connected. Authority, discretion and accountability qualify arrangements rather than collapse into actor labels. Strategies, architectures, regimes and governance logics are higher-order constructs and may coexist.

V8 also separates architecture from regulatory regime. Regimes may contain several architectures and be nested, embedded, layered, overlapping, cross-cutting, coupled or latent. Architecture within regimes concerns the organization of actors and relationships; architecture among regimes concerns relations among specialized regimes.

## 5. The proposed morphological–relational method

The method is eight linked operations: delimitation; decomposition; relational reconstruction; configurational reconstruction; positional analysis; part–whole analysis; architectural inference; and boundary/sensitivity analysis. It starts with a declared source and problem, preserves institutional statements even where no relation is warranted, and makes every higher-order claim traceable to legal evidence. It is a project method hypothesis, not an attribution to V8.

## 6. From legal asymmetries to functions, mechanisms, instruments and relationships

Legal provisions often contain asymmetric assignments of duty, competence, entitlement or prohibition. A duty on a controller is not automatically a relation to a regulator. The statement should be retained as a normative assignment unless a counterparty and governance-relevant connection are evidenced. Conversely, one relationship can be constituted across several provisions; all supporting statements must be linked.

This discipline prevents a generic graph from flattening legal meaning. It also preserves V8's distinction among function, mechanism and instrument, including cases where their boundary remains uncertain. The right response to an uncertain taxonomy is an explicit uncertainty state, not an invented definitive classification.

## 7. From observable elements to configurations

A configuration is a specified combination of relationships, functions, mechanisms, instruments and attributes organized around a governance problem or stage. It may overlap another configuration. Candidate grouping is not yet an architectural conclusion: a reporting duty does not establish a reporting system, and a shared actor does not establish an integrated whole.

The method therefore asks what legally qualified coupling joins components, how the arrangement allocates work and capacity, and whether a positional feature—such as an indispensable legal gateway—has actual support beyond network reachability.

## 8. From configurations to warranted architectural claims

An architectural claim states its problem and scale; the relations/configurations that support it; the relevant coupling; the configurational consequence and bearer; significance factors; alternatives; and boundary uncertainty. It stops at the highest supported level. Frequency cannot substitute for authority, centrality, exclusivity, sequence, consequence or substitutability.

This rule is deliberately conservative. A counterfactual removal exercise can aid sensitivity analysis but cannot be a universal criterion, because legal arrangements can be redundant or offer multiple paths.

## 9. Multidimensional properties and architectural comparison

V8's dimensions—centralization, intermediation, public–private allocation, self-regulatory autonomy, vertical distribution, decision-centre plurality, relational depth, functional allocation, discretion distribution and accountability structure—form a profile, not a score. A law can be intermediated in verification while centralized in sanctions, or multilevel without being polycentric. The method requires values to be stated per function, configuration and scale before any aggregate description.

## 10. Architectures within and among regulatory regimes

The same relationship may contribute to multiple candidate regimes. A many-to-many model preserves this possibility and records the relation claimed between regimes. It prevents statutory location or graph clustering from becoming an unexamined regime boundary. Latent functional regimes require a declared problem, structured roles and relationships, continuity and sufficient coherence; they are not established by a label.

## 11. Exploratory application: GDPR

The new GDPR pilot uses a verified historical EUR-Lex baseline: 99 operative articles, 389 paragraph units and 382 point units, with the 2018 corrigendum applied. All parsed operative units receive machine-first screening; a smaller set of source-linked curated observations supports the worked trace.

The cross-border cooperation-and-consistency trace shows the method's value. Article 56 allocates lead authority competence; Article 60 organizes cooperation, draft circulation and a conditional referral; Article 63 identifies the consistency mechanism; Article 65 gives the Board a binding dispute-stage decision while requiring the lead authority or relevant complaint authority to adopt the final decision on its basis. The sequence supports a narrow configuration-level account of distributed first-instance authority and concentrated dispute-stage authority. It does not turn the Board into the final decision-maker toward the controller or processor, nor establish a whole-GDPR architecture.

## 12. Methodological limitations and validation requirements

The pilot is machine-assisted and exploratory. It does not establish current law, implementation, operational effects, independent validity, reliability or comparative generality. National law, external institutional bases and Treaty review remain outside the corpus. Future work needs independent coding, matched units, separate agreement assessment by level and human legal adjudication of ambiguous relationships and architecture claims.

## 13. Implications for the broader relational leximetrics research programme

The proposed contribution is not a new competing theory of governance. It is an operational discipline for V8's reconstructive ambition: preserve qualified legal relations, distinguish direct observation from inference, state property bearers and boundaries, and test whether arrangement adds information beyond inventory. Its value must be judged empirically—by whether it improves clarity, reproducibility or defensibility using the same legal evidence.

It also addresses three collaboration priorities. For **coordination**, a separate many-to-many interoperability layer can link provision-based institutional statements to other relational-leximetric records without altering their originating codebooks. For **theoretical innovation**, the method must show that decomposition, interfaces, situated positions, part–whole attribution or boundary analysis changes an evidentiary conclusion rather than merely its visual presentation. For **practical progress**, the GDPR trace demonstrates the full path from provision to a limited architectural implication and records where the evidence does not warrant a stronger claim.

## 14. Conclusion and future research agenda

Relational leximetrics makes it possible to ask how law structures governance rather than only what it requires. A morphological–relational method may sharpen that inquiry where it shows why a configuration, rather than a count, warrants a property claim. The next agenda is not further assertion: it is independent review of the codebook, full expert coding of the pilot, boundary-sensitive comparison across instruments, and cautious development of regime-architecture analysis.
