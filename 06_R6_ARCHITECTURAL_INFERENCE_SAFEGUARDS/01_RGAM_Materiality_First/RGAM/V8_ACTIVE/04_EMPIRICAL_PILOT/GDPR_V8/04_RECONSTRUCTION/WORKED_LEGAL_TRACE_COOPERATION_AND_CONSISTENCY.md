# Worked legal trace: cooperation and consistency

**Status:** exploratory V8-based legal reconstruction from the declared historical source. It is not independently validated and does not establish practice, the complete European data-protection regime, or an architecture of the entire Regulation.

## Legal sequence and coded roles

| Provision | Institutional statement / relation | V8 elements and attributes |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 56(1) | The lead supervisory authority is competent for cross-border processing under Article 60. | **Actors:** lead authority, concerned authorities, controller/processor. **Function:** authorization/designation. **Mechanism/instrument:** competence allocation / Article 60 procedure. **Authority:** conditional competence. |
| Art. 56(3) | A locally approached authority informs the lead, which decides within three weeks whether to handle the case. | **Function:** cooperation/coordination. **Relationship:** information plus procedural allocation. **Discretion:** a legally bounded handling choice. |
| Art. 60(1), (3) | The lead cooperates with concerned authorities and submits a draft decision to them. | **Function:** cooperation and decision preparation. **Mechanism:** draft circulation and objection. **Instrument:** Article 60 procedure. |
| Art. 60(4) | If the lead does not follow a relevant and reasoned objection, it submits the matter to the consistency mechanism. | **Relationship:** conditional procedural referral. **Mechanism:** escalation. The trigger is legal text, not graph connectivity. |
| Art. 63 | The consistency mechanism ensures consistent application of the Regulation. | **Function:** coordination/consistency. **Instrument:** consistency mechanism. It supplies a purposive institutional statement, not a free-standing architectural label. |
| Art. 65(1)(a), (2) | The Board adopts a binding decision on the qualifying dispute; that decision binds the supervisory authorities concerned. | **Function:** adjudication/dispute resolution. **Authority:** binding decision power at the dispute stage. |
| Art. 65(6) | The lead authority adopts its final decision on the basis of the Board's binding decision and notifies it. | **Relationship:** Board resolution structures, but does not replace, the lead authority's final decision toward the controller/processor. **Authority:** final decisional act remains with the lead. |
| Art. 64(1), (6) and Art. 66 | Opinion/standstill and exceptional-urgency provisional routes qualify the ordinary sequence. | **Functions:** review and urgent enforcement. **Mechanisms:** opinion, standstill, derogation. They demonstrate that the configuration is conditional and multi-path rather than a simple linear hierarchy. |

## V8 analytical reconstruction

**Candidate configuration:** cross-border cooperation-and-consistency decision arrangement.

**Scope:** cross-border processing in the declared historical GDPR source, with national authorities represented at the legal type level and the Board as constituted by the Regulation. National implementing law, the European Data Protection Supervisor's constitutive basis, Treaty review and operational practice are outside this boundary.

**Qualified coupling:** Article 56 activates Article 60 for cross-border processing; Article 60(4) conditionally links unresolved reasoned objections to Articles 63 and 65; Article 65(2) binds the authorities; Article 65(6) links that binding resolution to the lead authority's final decision. This is a legally specified sequence across relations.

**Configurational consequence:** within this defined arrangement, authority is distributed at first instance among supervisory authorities and becomes concentrated in the Board at a qualifying dispute stage, while the lead authority retains the final decisional act toward the controller or processor. The arrangement also includes an urgency route and opinion/standstill qualifications. This is a proposed configuration-level property with a limited preliminary architectural implication, not a claim that every GDPR function is organized this way.

**Attributes:** authority is directly evidenced in competence, binding decision and final-decision provisions. Discretion is only cautiously identified in the lead's Article 56(3) handling choice and must not be generalized across functions. The listed provisions do not by themselves establish a complete accountability relation: reporting, opinion and binding effect are not sufficient without the fuller answerability/assessment/consequence structure relevant to the precise claim.

**Alternatives and limits:** the cooperation stage (Arts. 56 and 60) and the consistency/dispute stage (Arts. 63–65) can be treated as distinct but coupled configurations. Article 66 creates a special route. Adding external national and Treaty sources could qualify instance-level and reviewability claims. The trace therefore stops at a source-linked, provisional reconstruction.
