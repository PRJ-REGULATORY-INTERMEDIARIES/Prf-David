# V6 manuscript: tracked editorial suggestions

This is an editorial copy of proposed changes. The original DOCX remains untouched at `Enviados/Artigo v6/draft v6  Law_and_Governance_Architectures_Revised.docx`. The markers are `[REPLACE]`, `[ADD]`, and `[COMMENT]`; no fictional graph or PILOT_03 artifact is used as legal evidence.

## 1. Abstract, V6 ¶6

**[REPLACE]** “This paper develops a framework for analyzing that neglected dimension.”

**With:** “This paper develops a proposed framework for making that dimension more explicit and traceable in comparative legal analysis.”

**[COMMENT]** “Neglected” is a literature-wide claim. Retain only after a documented review. The abstract should not imply that relational governance analysis begins here.

**[REPLACE]** “The broader contribution is to shift comparative legal analysis from asking primarily what law requires toward examining how law organizes the work and capacity of governing.”

**With:** “The proposed contribution is to complement analysis of what law requires with a traceable account of how legally constituted relationships organize governing work and capacity.”

## 2. Introduction, V6 ¶17–18

**[ADD after the paragraph introducing RIT, delegation, and orchestration]**

> These literatures already provide relational accounts of indirect governance, intermediaries, delegated authority, and distributed regulatory capacity. The present proposal should therefore be read as an operational synthesis: it asks how these relations can be anchored in legal text, coded with explicit provenance, and aggregated without treating a textual mention as proof of architectural centrality.

**[COMMENT]** This addition prevents the RIT framework, especially Abbott, Levi-Faur, and Snidal (2017), from being treated as merely contextual background to an unqualified novelty claim.

## 3. Ontology boundary, V6 ¶19 and the Level I–III table

**[REPLACE]** “At the most directly observable level”

**With:** “At a candidate low-level coding level, subject to interpretive decision rules,”

**[ADD below the table]**

> “Directly observable” refers to the presence of a text span and a defensible coding decision, not to theory-free observation. Actor type, function, mechanism, instrument, and relationship direction may require adjudication. Each coded item should retain the source span, modality, condition, temporal scope, domain, and adjudication status.

**[COMMENT]** This addresses organization-versus-position, conditional power, composite norms, and missing-link failures identified in `ONTOLOGY_STRESS_TEST.md`.

## 4. Relational leximetrics, V6 ¶23–25

**[REPLACE]** “The methodological contribution is what we call relational leximetrics.”

**With:** “The methodological proposal developed here is termed relational leximetrics.”

**[ADD after V6 ¶24]**

> The proposal does not claim priority over relational theories of regulatory intermediation, institutional grammars, network representations, or existing multi-level legal coding. Its intended contribution is the combination of (i) source-anchored relationship coding, (ii) explicit separation of coding, analysis, and inference units, and (iii) stopping rules for moving from legal text to architectural claims. The distinctiveness of that combination remains an empirical and comparative question.

## 5. Intermediation centrality, V6 ¶22 and ¶79–81

**[REPLACE]** “The latter concerns not only how frequently intermediaries appear but also the breadth of functions they perform, their authority, their indispensability, their structural position and the extent to which they are themselves regulated.”

**With:** “The proposal distinguishes textual prevalence from several candidate dimensions of intermediation: functional breadth, legally assigned authority, legally specified dependence, graph position in a declared representation, and regulation of the intermediary. These dimensions should not be collapsed into a single centrality score without validation.”

**[COMMENT]** “Indispensability” requires a counterfactual or explicit legal gatekeeping condition. It must not be inferred from degree, betweenness, or repeated mentions.

## 6. Formal, implementation, and operational architecture, V6 ¶26 and ¶85

**[REPLACE]** “Relational leximetrics reconstructs the system of authority, functions and relationships constituted by law.”

**With:** “Relational leximetrics is intended to reconstruct a coded representation of authority, functions, and relationships expressed or legally entailed by the text under stated rules.”

**[ADD]**

> A resulting graph or table is an analytical representation. It is not itself legal evidence, and it cannot establish implementation, operational effectiveness, compliance, legitimacy, or trust without additional empirical sources.

## 7. Conclusions, V6 ¶88–91

**[REPLACE]** “This approach opens a broader comparative research agenda. It allows researchers to ask whether...”

**With:** “If validated through primary-text demonstrations and comparative coding, this approach could support research asking whether...”

**[REPLACE]** “The broader proposition is simple.”

**With:** “The bounded proposition of this paper is that law can be studied not only as a set of directed rules but also as a source of formally constituted relationships that allocate governing capacity.”

**[COMMENT]** The narrower wording avoids converting a conceptual proposal into an established empirical result.
