Subject: A small possible extension to the V8 architecture discussion

Dear David,

I have read the latest V8 with great interest, especially its sharper distinction between governance architectures and regulatory regimes, including architectures among regimes. In parallel, I have been developing a modest morphological specification that may be useful as a complement to relational leximetrics rather than as a separate theory.

The proposal asks analysts to distinguish the properties of parts, qualified relationships, positions within a configuration, and the architectural whole; to keep normative assignments separate from evidenced actor-to-actor governance relationships; and to make the move from documentary corpus to reconstructed architecture explicit and auditable. I have attached a short executive note as entirely optional reading.

```text
legal evidence → positions / qualified relationships → subconfigurations → proposed architecture
      documentary boundary (W)                         ≠             inferred boundary (A)
```

I would be very grateful for your conceptual guidance on three questions:

**Q1.** Where, if anywhere, does this morphological decomposition misrepresent or unnecessarily narrow the architecture concept developed in V8?

**Q2.** Could making part-level, positional and whole-level properties explicit improve the reconstruction and comparison of governance architectures?

**Q3.** Which boundary criterion would you consider indispensable for identifying an architecture across multiple legal instruments?

I would welcome criticism, including the possibility that the proposed specification adds little beyond V8. Thank you for considering it.

Warmly,

Igor
