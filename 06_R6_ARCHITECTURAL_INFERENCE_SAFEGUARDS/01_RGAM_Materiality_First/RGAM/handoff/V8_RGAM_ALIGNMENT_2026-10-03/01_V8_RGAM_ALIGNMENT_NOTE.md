# V8–RGAM alignment note

**Purpose.** Internal scientific note for a preliminary consultation with David Levi-Faur. It is not a revision of either manuscript, an empirical validation, or a proposal that David has endorsed.

## 1. Sources examined

The primary baseline was David Levi-Faur, *Law and the Architectures of Governance: Towards a Relational Leximetrics Approach*, draft V8, 3 October 2026 (complete DOCX read). The relevant RGAM baseline was Igor Caires Machado, *From Architectural Configuration to Regulatory Governance Architecture*, v0.6.1, 3 October 2026, with its scientific revision register, primary-source verification matrix, theoretical-scope decision, candidate ontology v0.4 and decision log. The limited empirical illustration draws only on GDPR Pilot 01 records already present in the project.

## 2. What V8 now adds to the authorial baseline

**David's formulations.** V8 defines governance architecture as the legally constituted configuration through which governance work, authority, discretion and accountability are distributed among actors and organized through relationships, mechanisms and instruments. It treats architecture as an emergent, multidimensional and configurational property, not a label inferred from an actor, instrument, relationship or count. It also gives relationships a pivotal methodological role; separates units of coding, analysis and inference; requires explicit cross-level inference, vertical traceability and a stopping rule; and distinguishes formal legal, implementation and operational architectures.

V8 materially expands the regime discussion. Regulatory regimes are broader institutional systems than governance architectures and may extend beyond a law to standards, organizations, doctrines, practices and non-state institutions. Regimes may be nested, embedded, layered, overlapping, cross-cutting, coupled or meta-regulatory; some may be latent and functionally reconstructed. V8 consequently distinguishes architecture *within* regimes from architecture *among* regimes (regime architecture), and proposes many-to-many rather than exclusive membership as a future coding orientation. Governance logics remain distinct from strategies and architectures.

## 4. Redundancies and possible incompatibilities

RGAM should not claim priority for relational configurations, overlapping architectures, regime architecture, architectural visualization, intermediation, or the distinction between formal and operational arrangements. Those matters are already developed in V8, and architectural visualization of EU law has a close published precedent in Stratmann et al. (2026); the project has not critically read that article's full body.

The main possible incompatibility is scope. V8's regulatory governance includes public and non-state capacity, standards, contracts, market arrangements and intermediation. RGAM's first reconstruction protocol is restricted to the **formal legally constituted layer of potentially broader architectures**. That restriction must remain a research-design choice. It must not suggest that every governance participant originates in binding statutory law, nor that legal text exhausts a regime.

A second tension is parsimony. V8 already offers a rich inferential methodology. Extra RGAM fields are defensible only when they make an otherwise contestable attribution, boundary choice, or evidence-to-claim transition more auditable. The ontology should not be expanded merely because a representation can be made more elaborate.

## 5. Regime architecture and a future ontology amendment

V8 makes a regime-level extension intellectually plausible: an architecture can participate in several regimes, and the relations among regimes may themselves be architecturally comparative. RGAM should presently record this as an **inferential possibility**, not an achieved extension. A future G notation would require decisions about problem/scale, relation types among regimes, multiple membership, latent-regime inference, and boundary criteria. It should not be inserted into the frozen E–P–R–S–A–M–X ontology until David has assessed whether it clarifies rather than duplicates V8's regime architecture.

## 6. Consultation recommendation

Present RGAM as a modest proposed specification: it asks analysts to state what bears a claimed property, preserve qualified relationships and normative assignments as different observations, declare W separately from A, and leave the S→A inference contestable. The SC-07 GDPR trace may illustrate feasibility only: *Exploratory, machine-assisted legal illustration; not independently validated.* It does not test or validate the ontology, and it does not license broader claims about the GDPR or regulatory regimes.

### Matters requiring David's scientific adjudication

1. Does explicit bearer assignment clarify V8's multidimensional architecture, or merely restate it in unnecessarily ontological language?
2. What boundary criterion, beyond documentary inclusion and graph connectivity, should be indispensable before treating multiple legal instruments as one architecture?
3. Should the proposed G extension be developed as a distinct regime-architecture layer, and if so, at what inferential threshold?
