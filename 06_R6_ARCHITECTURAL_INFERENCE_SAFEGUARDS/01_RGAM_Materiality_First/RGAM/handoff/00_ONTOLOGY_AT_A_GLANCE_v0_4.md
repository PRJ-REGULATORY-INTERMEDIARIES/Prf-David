# A Materially Anchored, Configurational Ontology for Your Governance Architecture — at a glance

*Proposal for discussion, v0.4 rev3 · 3 October 2026 · prepared by Igor Caires Machado · not validated, not a codebook, no agreement on authorship or next steps is presumed.*

## The problem this addresses

Your V6 defines a governance architecture as the legally constituted configuration through which governance work, authority, discretion and accountability are distributed, and says that architecture is **emergent** — it cannot be read off a single actor, instrument or relation. V6 also warns that counts identify composition, not organization. What the paper does not yet specify is **what kind of thing each of the twelve categories is when we reconstruct an architecture**, which properties belong to a *part* and which to the *whole*, and **what evidence entitles us to say an architecture is empirically reconstructed** rather than merely designed.

This note proposes one concrete answer. It **keeps your definition, your three levels, the inference ladder and the stopping rule unchanged**; it specifies them.

## The proposed definition

> A **regulatory governance architecture** is a *bounded*, *time-indexed* configuration of legally constituted **positions** and **qualified relations** (with the **instruments** that form their interfaces), through which governance work, authority, discretion and accountability are distributed. Its properties arise from the **arrangement of relations**, not from an inventory of parts. It is reconstructed from **material inscriptions**; its formal constitution, institutional instantiation and enacted operation are analytically distinct.

## Where your twelve categories sit

| V6 category | Role in the reconstruction |
|---|---|
| **Actors** | Positions — the nodes. Role is relative to the relation (your relational premise) |
| **Instruments** | Three situations kept apart: (1) a **typed characteristic** of a relation; (2) a **juridically constituted device** when the source gives it identity and an operator or rules; (3) a **materially instantiated device**, which needs evidence beyond the legal text. Devices are not actors and need not be nodes of the actor-position graph; a certificate or a statute text is a documentary artifact, not a device |
| **Relationships** | The pivotal bridge: the reified, qualified relation |
| **Objects · Functions · Mechanisms** | Qualifiers of a relation — *not* nodes; Function, Mechanism and Instrument stay three separate slots |
| **Authority · Discretion** | Attributes of a relation; also **distributions** over the whole |
| **Accountability** | A qualified relation *and* a pointer that qualifies another relation or arrangement — one observation, referenced, never duplicated. Four classes: **information** (reporting/disclosure/publicity — never accountability); **answerability** (identifiable forum, duty to explain and justify, questioning capacity, but not all elements); **accountability** (also assessment/judgment capacity and a grounded possibility of consequences); **indeterminate**. Distributed public forums are admissible when their capacities are institutionally identifiable. Legally constituted capacities do not show that the arrangement operates |
| **Strategies** | Derived annotation of a set of relations, orthogonal to configuration |
| **Architectures** | The bounded whole; named forms are *patterns matched to subconfigurations*, as in your §5.2.3 |
| **Governance logics** | An interpretive horizon above the architecture |

Not every category becomes a node: an actor, a verb and a concept are not equivalent vertices.

## Parts versus whole

Four kinds of property, never interchanged without a stated rule: **intrinsic** (what the text confers on a position), **relational** (a relation's own qualifiers), **positional** (what a position *is within the whole*: a necessary stage, depth from the rule-maker) and **emergent** (distribution and structure across the arrangement — your ten dimensions in §5.2.1). Positional and emergent properties are exactly the ones your fallacies (atomistic, ecological) warn about.

## Materiality: four distinctions

Materiality is **not** a building, not "has a PDF", not implementation, and not just provenance. It concerns how a legally constituted architecture finds expression and support in identifiable **inscriptions** and **institutional devices**, and it constrains the warrant of a claim of *empirical reconstruction*.

- **Documentary inscription** — an authentic, versioned text or trace exists, even a draft or repealed one.
- **Legal constitution** — that text has legal effect for the stated version and interval.
- **Material instantiation** — the office is filled, the register exists, the platform runs, each with its own provenance (applies to positions and devices; a relation depends on them and carries a *support-gap report*).
- **Observed operation** — practice is observed (an adjacent layer; sociomateriality is a complementary lens, not the object).

Claim statuses: *projected · documented · legally constituted · materially instantiated · operationally observed*; each independently evidenced, none inferred from another, no fixed life cycle. A draft bill is documented, not constituted; a mandated, unbuilt platform is constituted, not instantiated; a legible PDF proves documentation only. This operationalizes your formal / implemented / operational distinction (V6 §6.13). We do **not** reduce existence to observability: an operating arrangement may exist undocumented.

## What it adds — a contrast (TOY / NOT REAL LEGAL EVIDENCE)

Same inventory — Authority X, Verifier V, Firm F — and the same functions in two invented laws.

- **α:** X accredits V; V verifies F; F reports to X; X may sanction F and V.
- **β:** V verifies F; F reports to V; V informs X only in aggregate; X sanctions F only on V's referral; X has no power over V.

"V verifies F" reads identically in both. Yet V is a **necessary stage** in β and not in α, and X's lines to F differ. No part-level coding distinguishes them; only the configuration does. This is the discriminating test we propose: *hold the inventory constant, vary the relations, see whether the architectural claim changes.* It draws on the configurational tradition of Hillier and Hanson as an **epistemological parallel** (transfer of analytic structure — part/whole properties and contrast design — not of space-syntax metrics), and on critical realism for the ontological frame. These attributions are still to be checked against the primary texts.

## Boundaries and exclusions

- Every architecture is stated for a **corpus × jurisdiction × domain × interval × text version**, with an explicit membership rule; a statute is not automatically one architecture (your container fallacy).
- A source document is not an actor; a platform is an instrument/interface, not an independent actor; an office is not its holder.
- An obligation on A does not make an A→B relation; silence is neither permission nor prohibition; a network drawing is not evidence.
- Not claimed: effectiveness, compliance, outcomes, a validated metric, or a single architectural score.

## Status and limits

A candidate ontology, **not validated**.
