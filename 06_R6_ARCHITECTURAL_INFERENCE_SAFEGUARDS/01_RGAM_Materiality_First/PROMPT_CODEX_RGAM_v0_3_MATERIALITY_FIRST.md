## 1. NON-NEGOTIABLE CONCEPTUAL DECISION: ARCHITECTURE, MATERIALITY AND SOCIOMATERIALITY

**Keep the axis of the study on the ontology and morphology of architecture, not on the implementation of regulatory governance.** Do not append sociomateriality as a sixth or seventh morphological category or recast this as an article primarily about sociomaterial practices.

Adopt the following conceptual distinctions as Igor's v0.3 working proposal, subject to critical stress-testing:

- **Architecture**: an identifiable, bounded and temporally situated configuration of distinguishable constituent positions, qualified relations, boundaries and interfaces, with properties that are not reducible to a simple inventory of isolated parts.
- **Materiality**: a constitutive requirement and an empirical anchor of architecture. An assertion about an empirically identifiable architecture must specify the legally, institutionally, documentary, technical or otherwise materially instantiated referents and evidence through which its constitution can be investigated. Materiality is NOT synonymous with a building, physical adjacency, mere presence of a PDF, or actual regulatory implementation. Digital inscriptions and artefacts have material infrastructures as well.
- **Morphology**: the central object of analysis: how the constituent parts and qualified relationships are organized into local arrangements, overlapping subconfigurations and a bounded whole.
- **Sociomateriality**: a relevant complementary lens for examining the constitutive entanglement of social/material dimensions, particularly in enacted practices and transformations. Do NOT inaccurately claim that sociomateriality means only implementation: Orlikowski's theoretical claim is broader. For the present article, delimit its operational use primarily to an external/future layer on enactment so it does not replace the architectural ontology.
- **Implementation / enactment**: a separate empirical question: whether and how normatively constituted arrangements are actually instituted, operated and transformed in practice. Do not deduce implementation or effectiveness solely from the existence of a legal text.

The precise philosophical assertion to test is: **Without a defensible material referent or material inscription, we may have an idea, ideal type or design of architecture, but we must not present it unqualified as an empirically reconstructed architecture.** Avoid naïve physicalism or the false proposition that institutional/legal facts are reducible to paper, hardware or buildings.

Make THREE materiality distinctions explicit:

A. **Material inscription/evidentiary trace**: enacted legal texts, official registers, authoritative records, versions, digital documents and other traceable inscriptions. The physical/digital existence of a source can support a claim about formal juridical constitution, but does not prove institutional installation.
B. **Institutional/technical instantiation**: evidence of constituted positions being filled/established, functioning organizations, operating registers/platforms, instruments and infrastructures where relevant. Must have its own provenance rather than being inferred automatically from a normative mandate.
C. **Enacted sociomaterial practice**: empirically observed use, interactions, appropriation, compliance and adaptation; important, but outside the constitutive morphological core of the first legal-corpus reconstruction.

Separate FOUR claim statuses as appropriate (avoid conflating ontological status with evidence confidence): `conceptually_projected`; `normatively_inscribed/formally_constituted`; `institutionally_instantiated`; `operationally_observed`. State explicitly that these statuses need not be a universal linear life cycle and should be assigned only with the required evidence. A draft bill is not an enacted law; a legally mandated but unbuilt platform is not an operational platform.

Suggested core formulation to refine critically (do not pass it off as a direct quotation from any source):

> Regulatory governance architecture is a bounded, temporally situated and materially anchored configuration of distinguishable regulatory positions, qualified relations, boundaries and interfaces, whose morphological properties arise from the arrangement of its constituent parts rather than from their enumeration alone. Its formal legal constitution, institutional instantiation and enacted operation are analytically distinguishable.

An optional framing thesis:

> Materiality anchors architecture in an identifiable reality; morphology makes its configuration analytically intelligible; sociomateriality offers a complementary perspective on how such configurations are enacted and transformed through practice.

Distinguish at least these analytical strata (adjust labels, but document any change):

- **E — Evidence and material inscriptions**: source document, provision, legal proposition, authority/version/date/status, provenance, accessible material trace. A source document is NOT automatically a regulator or architectural node.
- **P — Constituent parts**: organizations, legally specified institutional positions, roles/actor categories and regulatory instruments, each as distinct types; do not confuse an office with its holder.
- **R — Qualified relations**: grounded types, parties, direction when defensible, deontic modality, conditions, dates, juridical effect, and provenance. Allow n-ary/reified relations and distinguish `not found`, `expressly prohibited`, `conditional`, and `established`.
- **S — Subconfigurations**: theoretically/source-grounded sets of parts and relationships, capable of overlap or nesting.
- **A — Whole architecture**: the bounded morphology for an explicitly stated corpus, jurisdiction, domain and temporal interval.
- **M — Material anchoring (transversal, NOT another topological tier)**: which empirical inscription/artefact/institutional referent supports each claimed element, relationship or architectural status; identify its materiality level (A/B/C above), evidence type and limitations.
- **X — Adjacent analytical layers**: historical genesis, aims, sociomaterial enactment, implementation and performance/outcomes. Keep them available for future research without treating them as automatically constitutive morphological properties.

Preserve existing five candidate morphology families unless a documented discriminant test requires revision: (1) composition/distribution, (2) typed relational configuration, (3) authority stratification/distribution, (4) integration/segmentation, (5) interface/boundary permeability. Boundary/corpus/time delimitation is a prerequisite. These are property families, NOT validated metrics or one composite score. Specify clearly whether any property belongs to a part, relationship, subconfiguration, or the whole.

For every ontology class, provide: precise definition, inclusion/exclusion rules, identifying evidence, material anchoring where applicable, scale, relation to adjacent classes, and one non-trivial counterexample.
