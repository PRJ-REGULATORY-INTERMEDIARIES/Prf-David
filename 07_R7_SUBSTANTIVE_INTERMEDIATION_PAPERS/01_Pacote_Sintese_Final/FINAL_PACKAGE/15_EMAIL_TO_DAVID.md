**Subject:** Operationalizing the inference ladder — a short note

---

Dear David,

I tried to operationalize the inferential sequence in your paper — text to statements to relationships to configurations to architecture — on four EU instruments, with AI doing the coding. It turned out to be far more informative about method than I expected, and not in the way I anticipated.

The attached note explains what worked, what did not, and where I think I overbuilt. The short version: reading the provisions was the easy part, recording the relations was less safe than I assumed, and the real trouble is the step up to architecture, where the characteristic error is the container fallacy you already name in §6.8. I tested some safeguards against it. Most of the apparatus I built around them should go.

One result I would particularly like your view on sits in §9. The chain that governs the intermediaries themselves — accreditation, designation, supervision, withdrawal — is recoverable from legal text, and its *location* differs between instruments: the AI Act constitutes it internally, CBAM and Directive 86/361 largely refer it out. That may be a structural variable worth building a comparative design around, which would be a way of taking your question about who governs the governors somewhere empirical.

This is all preliminary and I have been explicit about the limits — only six of the fifty-six recorded disagreements were assessed at all, and I made those six blinded judgments myself, which is the weakest point in it. I would rather show you the thin version now than a more polished one later.

No urgency at all. Happy to talk whenever suits you.

Warm regards,
Igor
