## 8. David's four priority questions on regulatory intermediation

**Status: RESEARCHER-SUPPLIED — not archived in this workspace.**

Searched for the question wording and near-variants across the repository: no match. The four questions used in Stage 6 are taken from the researcher's instruction:

1. What makes an intermediary structurally important?
2. How are intermediaries themselves governed?
3. When does intermediation complement rather than substitute for hierarchy?
4. What explains variation in intermediation across laws and regulatory sub-regimes?

They are, however, strongly continuous with David's own manuscript, which is archived: §6.12 treats intermediation centrality as a six-dimensional property (prevalence, functional breadth, authority, dependency/indispensability, structural position, regulatory embeddedness); the conclusion asks "who governs those who themselves govern"; and §6.12 explicitly separates intermediation centrality from enhanced self-regulation. Stage 6 therefore grounds each question in the manuscript rather than in the prompt alone.

## 10. Unresolved provenance issues

1. **Fifty of the 56 cards were never human-adjudicated** (§6, §7). The six that were assessed are archived as of 2026-10-08 (sources #25–#29), but without justifications, and not as a protocol-compliant freeze (DEV-16-01, DEV-16-02). The bulk of the Round-1 evidence therefore remains *pre-adjudication* and explicitly provisional. `12_CLAIMS_AUDIT.md` marks every claim that depends on the six.
2. **Project memory was stale.** The stored note said selection was blocked on 2026-10-05 and that the baseline arm had not started. In fact both arms, the preregistration, Round 1 and the post-adjudication preparation were completed on 2026-10-06/07. The memory file should be corrected; this package does not depend on it.
3. **Disclosed Round-1 incidents** (source #22): a stale deny rule on the baseline arm path; digest-only access to five files under a forbidden path (contents not opened); heterogeneous baseline manifest schemas, with the CBAM baseline manifest lacking a legal-source row (the identical hash is in its provenance record); an interruption with same-context recovery; and two self-corrections made before freezing. None alters a comparison category, and none is concealed in the note.
4. **Comparator asymmetry is not measured.** Both arms and the comparator ran on `claude-sonnet-5-5`; reasoning effort was not exposed to the executor. The exercise therefore cannot separate "effect of the added controls" from "effect of a longer, more structured prompt on the same model." This limit is stated in the note and is a design task for the next phase.
5. **Materiality was operationalized by the comparator.** The frozen rule was applied, but its operationalization (informational/administrative relations treated as non-material) is the comparator's own and is logged as such. A reviewer who disagrees can re-weight units without changing any M category.
