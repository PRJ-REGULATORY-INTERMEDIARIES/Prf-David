# 04 — Four-case comparative synthesis

**What this is.** A case-by-case account of what the paired reconstructions converged on, where they diverged, and what the added controls changed. No composite scores. No methodological winner. The comparison is between two *procedures for applying David's framework*, not between two frameworks.

**Evidence status.** All counts come from the frozen Round-1 registers. Six cards were assessed under blinding and are archived in `NEW TEST 3x3/16_HUMAN_VALIDATION_IMPORT_2026-10-08/`, without recorded justifications; the other 50 were not human-adjudicated and are not treated as adjudicated evidence.

---

## Case 1 — Regulation (EU) 2023/955, Social Climate Fund

### Case
EU funding instrument. 29 articles, five annexes. Member-State plans financed from the Fund against milestones and targets.

### Main reconstruction
Both arms: the Member State submits a Social Climate Plan; the Commission assesses it and decides by implementing act; an agreement follows; payments are conditioned on achievement of milestones and targets; financial-control and audit duties attach; monitoring, reporting and evaluation run on a shared indicator set; transfers to and from shared-management programmes are possible.

### Where the two procedures converge
26 of 57 comparative units substantively equivalent, including 9 of 11 at the evidence level. The plan cycle, the conditionality of payment, and the actors are not in dispute.

### Main difference
Architecture boundary. Baseline: **one** bounded architecture covering Arts. 1–27 and Annex III. Controlled: **one** integrated architecture (plan, payment, control, transfers) **plus** monitoring/evaluation as an overlapping candidate joined through a shared indicator interface **plus** the Parliamentary social climate dialogue as an indeterminate candidate for which no architecture is asserted. 8 of 22 material differences sit at C3 — the heaviest architectural concentration in the family.

### What additional control changed
The coupling test (I2/I3) and the candidate-status rule. A grouping could not become an architecture by being written down; it had to show which property becomes attributable to the whole rather than to each part. Monitoring/evaluation failed to become fully integrated; the Parliamentary channel failed I3 and I4 altogether.

### Whether it altered substantive interpretation
Yes. The number and boundaries of architectures in the instrument differ, which changes what the case is an instance of. It did not alter any statement about what the Regulation requires of whom.

### Human-validation evidence
HQ-SCF-02 (C3; H5/H6/H11; HIGH): reported as supporting the controlled reconstruction. Archived 2026-10-08; no justification recorded.

### Remaining uncertainty
Whether monitoring/evaluation is inside or alongside the architecture; whether the Parliamentary channel is an architecture at all; what confidence either claim deserves (D6 indeterminate in 20 of 22 material units); 13 unverified constitutive external references.

### Methodological lesson
The container default is the live risk in a well-drafted single-purpose instrument. A test that asks what property attaches to the whole, rather than which provisions sit in the same act, changes the answer — and in this case the reported decision endorsed the restraint.

## Case 2 — Regulation (EU) 2023/956, CBAM

### Case
Carbon border adjustment. 36 articles, six annexes. Authorised declarants, accredited verifiers, a registry, tradable certificates, a transitional reporting regime.

### Main reconstruction
Both arms: declarants are authorised and may lose that status; embedded emissions are declared and verified by accredited verifiers; a registry records positions; certificates are bought and surrendered; the Commission makes delegated and implementing rules; enforcement and review follow.

### Where the two procedures converge
27 of 70 units equivalent, including **14 of 15 at the evidence level** — the strongest textual agreement in the family.

### Main difference
Two differences, at different levels.
1. **C1, actor identity.** Baseline: certificates are "priced, sold, held, surrendered and repurchased or cancelled through a platform and the registry, with consequences for non-compliance; **the seller is not identified**." Controlled: a Member State sells certificates to authorised declarants established in it and repurchases excess certificates on request; the Commission establishes and manages the platform, registers each sale, repurchases on behalf of the Member State and cancels remaining certificates annually. Art. 20(1): "A Member State shall sell CBAM certificates on a common central platform to authorised CBAM declarants established in that Member State."
2. **C3, architecture boundary.** Baseline: one architecture over the whole design, medium-high confidence. Controlled: an integrated core (authorisation, certificates, obligations, enforcement) with verification and delegation overlapping but indeterminate in coupling, and the transitional regime, circumvention and reporting arrangements not coupled to it; 4 of 7 candidates `INDETERMINATE`; the whole-instrument candidate `OVERLAPPING_OR_PARTIALLY_INTEGRATED` with I2–I4 indeterminate.

### What additional control changed
For (1), actor/counterparty precision: no relation without identifiable named parties, and no generic or implied counterparty. For (2), the same coupling and candidate-status controls as in SCF.

### Whether it altered substantive interpretation
Yes, in both. (1) is a change in the content of a governance relation: who sells is not a detail in a market-access instrument, and the baseline left it blank. (2) changes the architectural boundary.

### Human-validation evidence
HQ-CBAM-04 (C1; H1/H2/H10; HIGH): reported as supporting the controlled reconstruction.
HQ-CBAM-10 (C3; H5/H6/H11; HIGH): reported as supporting the controlled reconstruction.
Both archived 2026-10-08; no justification recorded.

### Remaining uncertainty
Whether verification is architecturally integrated into the core — the controlled arm's own verification candidate is `INDETERMINATE` on I3/I4, so this is open on both sides. 17 unverified constitutive external references, two of them load-bearing for the verifier's status.

### Methodological lesson
HQ-CBAM-04 is the one validated instance where a control fixed something at the relational level rather than restraining an inference. It is a reminder that precision controls and restraint controls are different things and earn their place for different reasons. The restraint controls operated here exactly as in SCF.

## Case 3 — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, AI Act

### Case
The largest instrument in the family: 113 articles, 13 annexes. Risk-tiered obligations, conditional third-party conformity assessment, notified bodies, harmonised standards, national market surveillance with a Union safeguard, and a separate regime for general-purpose AI models.

### Main reconstruction
Both arms: provider and deployer duties across the high-risk lifecycle; conformity assessment, internal or by a notified body depending on the route; designation and supervision of notified bodies; harmonised standards and presumptions of conformity; national market surveillance with Union review and safeguard; Commission-centred supervision of general-purpose models.

### Where the two procedures converge
30 of 85 units equivalent, including 12 of 13 at the evidence level and 6 of 18 at the architectural level. Both arms identify the same three substantive pathways.

### Main difference
Two differences, and they point in opposite directions.
1. **C3, whole-level property.** Baseline: one bounded architecture, "differentiated, hybrid and multilevel," over the three pathways, **high confidence**. Controlled: five integrated candidates plus three indeterminate, and for the instrument as a whole I3 `NOT_SATISFIED` — **no property attributable to the Regulation as such**.
2. **C2, configuration partition — the counterexample.** Baseline: three arrangements, with designation of conformity-assessment bodies placed **with market surveillance**, and incident and information duties placed there too. Controlled: seven separate overlapping arrangements, with designation grouped **with certification**.

### What additional control changed
For (1), the whole-level property rule and the coupling test. For (2), the subconfiguration layer: 19 subconfigurations where the baseline had 3 configurations, and 26 of 85 units were controlled-arm-only propositions.

### Whether it altered substantive interpretation
Yes for (1): whether a property can be predicated of the Regulation at all is a substantive architectural question, and the arms answer it oppositely. Yes for (2), but not in the controlled arm's favour.

### Human-validation evidence
HQ-AI-14 (C3; H5/H6/H11; HIGH): reported as supporting the controlled reconstruction.
HQ-AI-12 (C2; H4/H6; HIGH): reported as supporting **the baseline** reconstruction.
Both archived 2026-10-08; no justification recorded.

**The counterexample deserves its own statement.** HQ-AI-12 is the only one of the six validated cards at the configuration level, and the only one in which the baseline prevailed. The reported decision placed designation of notified bodies with market surveillance rather than with certification. Note what this means for the controls: the finer subconfiguration layer did not resolve the membership question. It deferred it, and the controlled arm then resolved it at the architecture layer by grouping SC-01, SC-02, SC-03 and SC-18 as one integrated candidate — i.e. designation with certification. The decomposition produced more intermediate objects without producing a better membership judgment.

### Remaining uncertainty
The correct partition beyond this one decision. **39 constitutive external references** — the highest in the family — none verified. 30 source-ambiguity entries. D6 indeterminate in all 36 material units.

### Methodological lesson
Two lessons, and they must be held together. Where the baseline's confidence was highest, the restraint control had the most to constrain, and the reported decision endorsed it. Where the question was *which relations belong together*, more decomposition did not help. Granularity and restraint are not the same virtue, and this case separates them.

## Case 4 — Council Directive 86/361/EEC

### Case
The smallest and oldest instrument: 12 articles, no annexes, repealed in 1992. The initial stage of mutual recognition of type approval for telecommunications terminal equipment.

### Main reconstruction
Both arms, identically: the Commission requests CEPT to draw up specifications as NETs; NETs are regarded as the equivalent of common conformity specifications; approved testing laboratories test and certify; a certificate of conformity with a published specification removes the need for further tests; competent authorities recognise certificates for type approval; recognition may be suspended, with consultation, a laboratory opinion, Committee referral, delisting and revocation.

### Where the two procedures converge
**30 of 52 units equivalent — 58%, the highest in the family**, including 13 of 16 at the relational level. Only 9 of 22 differences were material, the lowest in the family, and 4 of those 9 are at the epistemic level C4 rather than in the substance.

### Main difference
Whether the architectural level may be asserted at all. Baseline: a bounded formal legal architecture for conformity assurance and mutual recognition of the covered process, **high confidence**. Controlled: both candidates `INDETERMINATE`, instrument-level result `INDETERMINATE`, and **highest level reached = S** — the only case in the family where the controlled arm stopped below architecture.

### What additional control changed
External-dependency marking, together with the stopping rule as an explicit output. Four constitutive external references, two load-bearing: a NET's legal constitution rests on a Memorandum of Understanding between CEPT signatories; which laboratories count as "approved testing laboratories" is fixed by the CEPT accreditation system. The Directive's own Art. 2(8) defines an approved laboratory by reference to conformity with that external system. The arm's reasoning: an architecture whose central performers and central specifications are constituted elsewhere is not an architecture the Directive constitutes.

### Whether it altered substantive interpretation
Yes, and more sharply than in any other case. The arms describe the mechanism identically and reach opposite conclusions about whether the instrument constitutes an architecture.

### Human-validation evidence
HQ-DIR-01 (C3; H5/H8/H9/H10/H11; HIGH; external dependency YES): reported as supporting the controlled reconstruction. Archived 2026-10-08; no justification recorded.

### Remaining uncertainty
What the external instruments provide — none was retrieved, and retrieval was not authorized. D1 on material units records **no** `SUPPORTED` status at all in this case.

### Methodological lesson
This is the cleanest demonstration in the pilot that the controls can change the top-level conclusion without changing the description. It also shows that the baseline was not careless: its own stopping record acknowledged that the Directive does not establish the significance or substitutability of the laboratories, and it asserted the architecture anyway. The difference between the arms is not attention. It is that one procedure had a field in which "stop below architecture" was an available answer.

## What the four cases jointly show

1. **Agreement is concentrated at the bottom of the ladder, disagreement at the top.** One material difference at the evidence level, across four instruments. This replicates in all four cases and is the most robust observation in the pilot.
2. **The architectural boundary question recurred in every case.** Three cases produced a whole-act-versus-several-architectures card; the fourth produced a whether-any-architecture card. This is David's container fallacy appearing as the default behaviour of an AI-assisted run, four times out of four.
3. **The effect of the controls scales inversely with instrument size.** On the 12-article Directive the arms agreed on 58% of units and differed decisively on one inference. On the 113-article AI Act they agreed on 35% and the controlled arm generated 1,163 records. The controls' procedural cost grows with the instrument; their analytical contribution does not obviously grow with it.
4. **Restraint and decomposition came apart.** Four validated endorsements of restraint at the architectural level; one validated non-endorsement of decomposition at the configuration level. That is the pilot's most useful distinction and the basis of the simplification proposed in `05_METHOD_CONTROLS_REVIEW.md`.
5. **Nothing here is a performance result.** No case was adjudicated. Six cards of 56 were validated, purposively chosen, four of them at one level. The comparison establishes where the difficulty lies, not which procedure is better.
