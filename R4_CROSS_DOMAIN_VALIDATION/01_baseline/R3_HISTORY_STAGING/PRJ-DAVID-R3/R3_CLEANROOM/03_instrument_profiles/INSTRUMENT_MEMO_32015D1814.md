# 1. Executive summary

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1-5]** This Decision establishes a market stability reserve for the Union greenhouse-gas emission trading scheme and makes targeted amendments to Directive 2003/87/EC.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 1-3]** Its regulatory system measures the total number of allowances in circulation, applies predefined thresholds and percentages, adjusts auction supply, and subjects the reserve to monitoring and periodic review.

**[ANALYTICAL_INFERENCE]** The declared orientation is a market-stabilization instrument within a wider EU ETS market-organization framework: it acts on allowance supply timing and quantity rather than directly regulating installation emissions.

# 2. Declared regulatory objective

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Recitals 4-6 and 11; Articles 1 and 3]** The declared objective is to address structural supply-demand imbalances, make the EU ETS more resilient, enable an orderly market and establish an operational reserve at Union level.

# 3. Regulatory object

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 1(4)-(8)]** The regulatory object is the quantity and timing of EU ETS allowances placed into or released from a reserve and the corresponding annual auction volumes.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 1-3]** The object also includes the data publication, auction-calendar and review arrangements required to make the supply adjustment predictable and administrable.

# 4. Principal target classes

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1-3]** The principal governed classes are Member States responsible for auction volumes and shares, the Union EU ETS market infrastructure, and market participants whose allowance supply conditions are altered by reserve operations.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 2]** Installations and persons governed by the underlying Directive are affected through amended auction and allowance-validity rules, but this Decision does not recreate their full Directive-level obligations.

# 5. Principal regulatory authorities

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1(4), 2 and 3]** The Commission publishes the total number of allowances in circulation, monitors the reserve, reviews its functioning and may submit legislative proposals. Member States adjust auction calendars and their auction volumes in accordance with the Decision.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 2-3]** The European Parliament and Council are the Union legislative institutions receiving proposals and participating in the amended Directive framework. Auction platforms are identified as operational infrastructure, not assigned relation-specific roles.

# 6. Legal scope

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1-4]** Material scope is the Union EU ETS allowance supply system, including reserve placement, release, auction calendars, specified unallocated allowances and the consequential amendments to Articles 10 and 13 of Directive 2003/87/EC.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1 and 4-5]** Territorial scope is Union-wide, operating through Member State auctioning. The reserve is established in 2018 and operates from 1 January 2019; special treatment applies to 2019-2020, while the transitional auction rule continues until 31 December 2018.

# 7. Major obligation clusters

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Article 1(1)-(3)]** Reserve constitution: the reserve is established, the 900 million allowances deducted during 2014-2016 are redirected, and specified unallocated allowances are placed in the reserve in 2020.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Article 1(4)-(8)]** Rule-based supply adjustment: the Commission publishes circulation totals; 12% is placed into the reserve when the threshold applies; 100 million is released when the lower threshold or Article 29a condition applies; auction calendars are adjusted over twelve months.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 2]** EU ETS alignment: Directive provisions on auctioning, end-of-period supply and replacement of invalid allowances are amended to integrate the reserve.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 3]** Monitoring and review: the Commission reports on the reserve, reviews it within three years of operation and every five years thereafter, and examines market balance, competitiveness, growth, jobs and carbon leakage.

# 8. Main governance components

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 1-3]** The components are: circulation measurement and publication; quantified reserve stock; threshold-based supply adjustment; auction-calendar administration; legal integration with the EU ETS; Commission monitoring and periodic review.

# 9. Structural logic

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 1-3]** allowance circulation publication -> threshold and percentage calculation -> reserve placement or release -> adjusted auction calendars -> market-function monitoring -> periodic review and possible legislative amendment.

# 10. Declared regulatory orientation

**[ANALYTICAL_INFERENCE — Articles 1-3]** Market-stabilization instrument. The Decision's central legal operation is a predictable, formulaic adjustment of allowance supply intended to improve the order and resilience of the EU ETS market.

# 12. Analytical uncertainties

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT / ANALYTICAL_UNCERTAINTY — Article 2]** The Decision relies on Directive 2003/87/EC and Commission Regulation 1031/2010 for the wider auction architecture. This memo does not import the full content of those instruments.

**[ANALYTICAL_UNCERTAINTY]** The label “market-stabilization instrument” describes the Decision's declared structure; it is not derived from frequency counting or from later empirical market effects.
