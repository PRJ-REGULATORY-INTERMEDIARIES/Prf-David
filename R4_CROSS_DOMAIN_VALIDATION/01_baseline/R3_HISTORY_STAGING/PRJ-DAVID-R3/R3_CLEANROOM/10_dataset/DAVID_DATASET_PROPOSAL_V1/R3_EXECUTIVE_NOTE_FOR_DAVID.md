# R3 Dataset Proposal — Executive Note

Dear David,

I rebuilt the five-instrument pilot through an independent, auditable reconstruction to address a basic design problem: the project contained several relevant dimensions, but they did not all belong at the same analytical level. The resulting proposal separates the law, its actors, the institutional intermediary, the regulatory relation, the elementary mechanism event, and the configuration among mechanism events. A future regulatory-capacity layer remains separate and unpopulated.

The main question is:

> How are regulatory intermediation mechanisms organized and combined across actors and regulatory relations in EU climate regulation, and what regulatory capacities do these configurations appear designed to provide?

The current pilot demonstrates the first part descriptively. The second is enabled by the schema but remains open.

The five-case reconstruction contains 89 LAW × ACTOR records and 56 adjudicated relations: 24 direct R–T relations, 23 legally relevant non-RIT relations, and 9 supported intermediation relations. These nine relations resolve into 7 LAW × INTERMEDIARY units and 10 mechanism events: 4 `VERIFICATION`, 3 `EVALUATION_ASSESSMENT`, and 3 `IMPLEMENTATION`. Eight relations contain one mechanism. One contains an evidenced sequence from `VERIFICATION` to `EVALUATION_ASSESSMENT`. One law, `32015D1814`, is retained as a substantive zero for intermediation rather than treated as missing data.

The original nine-mechanism vocabulary could not classify three supported relations involving material programme implementation without stretching `TRANSLATION`. After adversarial review, I retained the relations and provisionally added `IMPLEMENTATION` as a tenth elementary mechanism. This is my decision (`R3-D08-IMPLEMENTATION`), not a mechanism attributed to you. It remains family-unassigned, and the full sequence from initial uncertainty to researcher adjudication is preserved.

The proposed human-facing workbook uses LAW × INTERMEDIARY as the principal comparative unit, with relations and mechanism events shown as child records. This allows one intermediary to act in several target-specific relations without turning those relations into a false chain, and it allows one relation to contain several mechanisms without compressing them into a compound label.

The pilot can validate the schema, calibrate the codebook, identify possible configurations, and generate hypotheses. It cannot establish EU-wide prevalence, causal effects, robust associations, validated regulatory capacities, or governance outcomes.

I would especially value your reaction to five questions:

1. Does the distinction between LAW × INTERMEDIARY, regulatory relation, mechanism event, and mechanism configuration capture the levels the dataset should distinguish?
2. Is the definition of an elementary mechanism at the right level of abstraction?
3. Does provisional `IMPLEMENTATION` belong at mechanism level, or should those cases be conceptualized differently?
4. Does the architecture move sufficiently from coding-driven classification toward research-question-oriented data design?
5. Should the next theoretical step derive higher-order regulatory capacities from mechanism configurations, and which capacities would be most important to distinguish?

The package includes a detailed memo, two workbooks, a method pipeline, the mechanism codebook, the normalized architecture proposal, and concise provenance materials.
