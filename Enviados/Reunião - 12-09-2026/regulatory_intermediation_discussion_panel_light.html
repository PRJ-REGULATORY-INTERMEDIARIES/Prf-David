Regulatory Intermediation Discussion Panel
R–I–T coding discussion

Where should we draw the boundary of intermediation?

This panel is intended as a conversation aid rather than a fixed codebook. The aim is to test possible interpretations, compare borderline cases, and record the choices that should guide later coding.

Working idea

A third actor may be treated as an intermediary when it performs a recognizable function that is connected to the regulatory relationship between R and T.

The key issue is not necessarily whether the actor has binding authority, but whether its role matters to how regulation is produced, transmitted, monitored, verified, interpreted, or implemented.

Possible starting point

A softer formulation

“Could we treat intermediation as a functional connection to the R–T relationship, rather than requiring formal authority over the target?”
Main boundary

What seems most important to clarify

When does a third actor move from being merely relevant to the regulatory environment to being part of the regulatory relationship itself?

Borderline cases

These are discussion examples meant to reveal where the conceptual boundary becomes difficult.
Case 1

Possible interpretation: this may be a relatively strong intermediary case because the third actor's output is formally built into the regulatory process, even if its conclusion is not binding.

Case 2

General scientific information

A scientific organization publishes research that the Commission may consult.

Possible interpretation: probably not enough by itself. A more specific institutional connection may be needed.

Case 3

Target self-reporting

Member States submit their own emissions data directly to the Commission.

Possible interpretation: usually a direct R–T reporting relationship rather than intermediation.

Case 4

Independent verifier

An operator must have emissions data checked by an accredited verifier.

Possible interpretation: a clearer intermediary case because a distinct actor performs a recognized verification function.

Case 5

Scientific advisory body

A board evaluates climate progress and gives recommendations to the Commission.

Possible interpretation: likely depends on how formally the advisory function is embedded in the regulatory process.

Case 6

Public consultation

Civil society organizations submit observations before the Commission adopts an act.

Possible interpretation: participation alone may be too broad unless the actor has a more defined regulatory function.

Case 7

Regulator-facing intermediary

An expert body never directs the target but provides formal assessments to the regulator.

Possible interpretation: direct interaction with T may not be necessary if the actor has a meaningful role in how R assesses or governs T.

Possible decision logic

A simple sequence for discussion. Each step can be softened or revised after the meeting.
Step 1

Find the R–T relation

Is there a recognizable regulatory relationship to begin with?

Step 2

Look for a distinct third actor

Is there someone or some body beyond R and T?

Step 3

Identify the function

What does that actor actually do in regulatory terms?

Step 4

Test the connection

How does that function matter to the R–T relationship?

Possible practical test
“Can we explain, from the legal text, how this third actor contributes to the way R regulates, evaluates, monitors, verifies, influences, or supports T?”
