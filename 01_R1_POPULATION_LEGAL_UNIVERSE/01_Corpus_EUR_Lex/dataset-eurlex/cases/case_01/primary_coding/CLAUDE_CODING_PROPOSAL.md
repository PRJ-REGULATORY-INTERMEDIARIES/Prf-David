# Claude Primary Coding Proposal — case_01

## A. Case overview

- **Act:** Regulation (EU) 2021/1119 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 30 June 2021 establishing the framework for achieving climate neutrality and amending Regulations (EC) No 401/2009 and (EU) 2018/1999 ("European Climate Law")
- **CELEX:** 32021R1119
- **Corpus hash confirmed:** `b24a02ea241ca631b606660bb48bb5a2222b29f849c4e4552fb99f871b2e8fd4` (matches `cases/CASE_REGISTRY.yaml`)

**Regulatory architecture, one paragraph.** The European Climate Law is a framework act: it sets a binding 2050 Union-wide climate-neutrality objective and a binding 2030 net-emission-reduction target (Articles 1–2, 4), provides for a future 2040 target (Article 4(3)-(7)), and creates a science-advisory body, the European Scientific Advisory Board on Climate Change, by amending Regulation (EC) No 401/2009 (Articles 3, 12). Its principal operative machinery is a periodic Commission oversight cycle: the Commission assesses collective Member State progress and the consistency of Union measures (Article 6), separately assesses individual national measures and may issue recommendations to a given Member State, who must then notify and report back (Article 7), and bases these assessments on a defined set of inputs, including EEA assistance in preparing the assessments themselves and reports from the Advisory Board and the Commission's Joint Research Centre (Article 8). Softer provisions address adaptation strategies (Article 5), public participation (Article 9), and voluntary sectoral roadmaps (Article 10). Article 13 makes a long list of largely definitional amendments to Regulation (EU) 2018/1999 inserting the new climate-neutrality objective into that Regulation's existing governance vocabulary, one of which (Article 15(1), inserted by Article 13(6)(a)) independently reproduces a full Member-State-to-Commission long-term-strategy reporting obligation.

## C. Intermediated relations

**Only one relation was coded `intermediated` (case_01-rel-003).**

**case_01-rel-003 — Article 8(4), cf. Recital 37.** R = European Commission; I = European Environment Agency (EEA); T = Member States. Article 8(4) states that "the EEA shall assist the Commission in the preparation of the assessments referred to in Articles 6 and 7" — the periodic assessments of Member States' collective progress and of individual national measures that can trigger Commission recommendations (Article 7(2)) or "necessary measures" (Article 6(3)). This is a specific, textually mandatory institutional duty tied to preparing a named supervisory instrument, not a bare mention or generic scientific reference — it matches the pattern of "producing information institutionally integrated into R's supervision of T." Recital 37 reinforces this by singling out the EEA in the same terms ("The EEA should assist the Commission, as appropriate and in accordance with its annual work programme"). Confidence is medium rather than high because the corpus does not specify the substantive depth of the EEA's contribution (drafting vs. verifying vs. compiling inputs). This record is flagged `[reviewer_attention: conceptual_boundary]` because it sits on the assistance/intermediation line the production instructions identify as the hardest call in this construct, and the secondary reviewer should independently test the reasoning in the coder_note's self-check.

A closely related but structurally weaker candidate — the Advisory Board and the Commission's Joint Research Centre, whose reports are listed as one of several bases for the same Article 6/7 assessments (Article 8(3)(b)) — was **not** coded `intermediated`. Unlike the EEA, neither body carries an explicit "assist" duty in the text; they are simply named among several report sources that also include plainly non-mediating inputs (general statistics, IPCC evidence). This was coded `uncertain` instead (case_01-rel-004; see Section E).

## D. Important direct relations

- **Commission oversight cycle (case_01-rel-001, case_01-rel-002).** The core enforcement-adjacent architecture of the Act: the Commission periodically assesses (a) collective Member State progress and Union-measure consistency (Article 6), and (b) individual national measures, with the power to issue public recommendations to a non-compliant Member State, who must notify the Commission of intended follow-up and report on it in a subsequent national plan (Article 7). Both are direct R→T relations (Commission → Member States) with no demonstrated third-party mediator within the Articles themselves; the EEA's assistance in preparing the assessments that feed this cycle is captured separately as the single intermediated relation (case_01-rel-003).
- **Long-term strategy reporting (case_01-rel-006).** Article 13(6)(a) fully reproduces the amended Article 15(1) of Regulation (EU) 2018/1999, creating a clean, high-confidence direct Member-State-to-Commission reporting obligation for a 30-year long-term strategy consistent with the climate-neutrality objective. This is textually the cleanest direct relation in the corpus.
- **Sectoral roadmaps (case_01-rel-005).** Article 10 gives the Commission an engagement/monitoring role over sectors that voluntarily prepare climate roadmaps. Coded direct at low confidence because the voluntary, non-binding character of this mechanism makes it a genuinely borderline case of what counts as a "regulatory relationship" at all (see Section E).

## E. Borderline cases

- **case_01-unc-001 / case_01-unc-002 — Advisory Board's general advisory mandate (Article 3) and its input into the Commission's own 2040-target/GHG-budget exercise (Article 4(4)).** Classic advice-versus-mediation boundary: the Advisory Board provides general scientific advice/reports to "the Union" and its advice is taken into account by the Commission when setting the Union's own future target — neither is textually tied to a specific R-T pair with an external target such as Member States. Considered and rejected as intermediation candidates.
- **case_01-unc-003 / case_01-rel-004 — Advisory Board and JRC as possible intermediaries via Article 8(3)(b).** Discussed in Section C above; coded `uncertain` in the main matrix precisely because Condition D (demonstrated mediating function) could not be responsibly settled — being one of several listed report sources might be institutionally integrated information supply, or might be the excluded case of mere scientific reference. Flagged for secondary-reviewer attention, including a live risk of `possible_overcoding` if this reasoning is extended too readily to any cited information source.
- **case_01-unc-004 — National climate advisory bodies and the EEA-notification duty (Article 3(4); Article 12).** A Member State's *optional* domestic climate advisory body operates entirely within that Member State's own institutional architecture and does not mediate an EU-level relationship; the accompanying duty to "inform the EEA" if such a body is established is a bare notification with no documented regulatory consequence. Both considered and rejected.
- **case_01-unc-005 — EEA/Advisory Board internal governance (Article 12, new Article 10a(3) and (5) of Regulation (EC) No 401/2009).** Appointment of Advisory Board members by the EEA Management Board, and mutual consultation/information duties between the Advisory Board and the Management Board/Executive Director, are internal agency governance with no external regulated target. Rejected as an R-I-T candidate.
- **case_01-unc-006 — Comitology assistance (Article 13(7)(b)).** The Commission is "assisted by the Energy Union Committee" when adopting implementing acts on Member States' reporting format. This could resemble delegated/procedural intermediation, but comitology committees are typically composed of Member State representatives assisting the Commission's own exercise of implementing power — analytically different from independent third-party mediation. The composition/role of the Committee and the applicable comitology procedure are not reproduced in this corpus, so `external_context_required` is set to true and no relation was coded.
- **case_01-unc-007 — Unnamed target in Article 5(5) guidelines.** The Commission must adopt guidelines on managing physical climate risk in "projects and programmes for projects," but no addressee actor is named in the text (Member State authorities? programme managers? private project promoters?). `external_context_required` is set to true; no relation was coded for lack of an identifiable T.
- **case_01-unc-008 — Foundational objective-setting obligations (Article 2(2); Article 5(1)).** These name "the relevant Union institutions and the Member States" as joint duty-bearers for the climate-neutrality and adaptation objectives, but no distinct supervisory R separate from the duty-bearers themselves is identified within these provisions (the closest oversight mechanism is the Article 6 assessment, coded separately). Treated as not meeting the R-I-T structural test on their own, despite their centrality to the Act.
- **case_01-unc-009 — Unnamed recipient in Article 5(4).** Companion to case_01-rel-007: the recipient of Member States' updated adaptation-strategy information (submitted "under Article 19(1) of Regulation (EU) 2018/1999") is not named in this corpus. `external_context_required` is true; R left unresolved in the main-matrix record rather than filled by plausibility.
- **case_01-unc-010 — Forward-looking carbon-removal certification framework (Recital 22).** A recital narrates a Commission communication announcing a future certification/verification framework for carbon removals. Thematically close to the intermediation construct (certification, verification) but non-binding and non-operative as it stands in this corpus; not coded as a relation.

## F. Primary-coder caveats

- **Fresh-read disclosure.** This act (CELEX 32021R1119) was previously used in an earlier intensive methodological pilot for this project (Terra/Claude replications, a four-way comparison, blind reference adjudication). Per explicit instruction, this proposal was produced by reading the frozen corpus (`cases/case_01/corpus/act.md`) fresh, from the current production prompt, codebook, and schema only. No prior pilot output, comparison, log, manifest, or result for this act was opened, read, listed, grepped, diffed, or consulted at any point in this session, and none of the prohibited paths listed in the production instructions (Section 2) were accessed.
- **Framework-act character.** This is a framework/objective-setting act rather than an implementation-level regulation; much of its text (recitals, Article 1–2, Article 4's target-setting machinery, Article 9's public-participation provisions, most of Article 13's definitional cross-reference insertions into Regulation (EU) 2018/1999) does not present a codable R-I-T structure at all. The main matrix is deliberately narrow — 7 relations — reflecting that a large majority of the corpus is objective-setting, institutional housekeeping, or non-binding engagement rather than a regulatory relationship between identifiable actors.
- **Source-boundedness limits.** Several provisions reference reporting or procedural mechanisms fully defined only in Regulation (EU) 2018/1999 or in EU comitology procedure, whose substantive content is not reproduced in this corpus (Article 5(4)'s Article 19(1) reports; Article 13(7)(b)'s Energy Union Committee/Article 44(1) comitology). These were either coded `uncertain` with `external_context_required: true` (case_01-rel-007) or held out of the main matrix as uncertainty-log entries only (case_01-unc-006, case_01-unc-007, case_01-unc-009), rather than filled in by institutional knowledge of how Regulation (EU) 2018/1999 or comitology generally work.
- **Conceptual-boundary density.** Two of the seven main relations (case_01-rel-003, case_01-rel-004) sit directly on the assistance-versus-intermediation boundary the production instructions flag as the hardest call in this construct; one (case_01-rel-005) sits on the boundary of whether a voluntary, non-binding engagement mechanism constitutes a "regulatory relationship" at all. All three carry explicit `[reviewer_attention: conceptual_boundary]` tags and detailed self-check reasoning in their `coder_note` fields for independent secondary-reviewer testing.
- **No positive-finding pressure applied.** Per the task framing, the low count of `intermediated` relations (one) was not treated as a deficiency to correct; several plausible-looking intermediary candidates (Advisory Board's general mandate, national climate advisory bodies, comitology committee) were deliberately routed to `direct`/`uncertain`/the uncertainty log rather than forced into `intermediated`.
