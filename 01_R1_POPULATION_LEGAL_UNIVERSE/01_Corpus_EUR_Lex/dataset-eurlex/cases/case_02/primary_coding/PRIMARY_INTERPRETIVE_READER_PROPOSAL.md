## A. Case overview

**Act:** Regulation (EU) 2023/956 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 10 May 2023 establishing a carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM).
**CELEX:** 32023R0956

CBAM is a market-based, self-declaratory regime that makes importers of specified emissions-intensive goods (cement, electricity, fertilisers, iron and steel, aluminium, hydrogen) purchase and surrender "CBAM certificates" corresponding to the embedded emissions of those goods, mirroring the carbon cost the EU ETS imposes on equivalent domestic production. The architecture layers several distinct regulatory relationships on top of one another: (i) a licensing/authorisation gate through which an importer becomes an "authorised CBAM declarant" (Member State competent authorities), supported by narrow certifying inputs (a tax authority's certification, a bank guarantee); (ii) an annual declaration-and-certificate-surrender cycle policed jointly by the Commission (registry, price-setting, oversight, preliminary calculations) and competent authorities (final determinations, penalties), whose accuracy rests on a distinct verification layer performed by third-party accredited verifiers — both for the declarant's own declaration and, further upstream, for third-country installation operators who choose to register and disclose verified data; (iii) a customs layer that gatekeeps the "authorised-declarant-only" import rule at the border and feeds import data into the Commission's cross-checking and risk-analysis functions; (iv) an enforcement layer (penalties for non-surrender or unauthorised importation); (v) a parallel, lighter-weight transitional-period reporting regime (2023–2025) built on the same customs/reporting logic but without certificates; and (vi) a small number of international-facing provisions (third-country electricity market-coupling exemptions, circumvention monitoring) that sit at the edge of how the RIT model is usually applied to intra-Union regulator/regulatee relationships.

## C. Intermediated relations

**rel-002 — Tax authority certifies applicant's tax-debt status (Art 5(5)(d), 17(2)(a)).** The competent authority (R) decides whether to grant "authorised CBAM declarant" status to an importer/applicant (T). One of the eligibility criteria (Art 17(2)(a)) is the absence of serious/repeated infringements, including of "taxation rules." Article 5(5)(d) requires the application to include a certification from the applicant's Member State tax authority that it is not subject to an outstanding tax-debt recovery order. Third-actor test: **Distinction YES** (tax authority is a separate national body from the CBAM competent authority and from the applicant); **Function YES** (a specific certifying act is attributed); **Regulatory integration YES** (the certification supplies the evidentiary basis for one of the competent authority's Art 17(2)(a) eligibility criteria); **Evidence YES** (Art 5(5)(d) and Art 17(2)(a) are both operative text); **Counterfactual YES** (without it, the competent authority would have no documented basis to confirm the tax-related eligibility criterion). Coded intermediated at medium confidence given the narrowness of the function within the broader authorisation relation (rel-001).

**rel-003 — Verification of embedded emissions, declarant channel (Art 6, 7(5), 8, Annex VI).** The core CBAM compliance loop: the authorised CBAM declarant (T) self-declares embedded emissions to the Commission/competent authority (R), but Article 8(1) requires those emissions to be independently verified by an accredited verifier (I) applying the Annex VI verification principles before the declaration is submitted (Art 6(2)(d)). Third-actor test: **Distinction YES** (verifier is a separate accredited person, distinct from both declarant and Commission/competent authority — accreditation itself is a separate relation, rel-007); **Function YES** (Art 8, Annex VI attribute a specific verification function: professional scepticism, reasonable assurance, a verification report); **Regulatory integration YES** (Art 7(5) explicitly ties the declarant's records to enabling both the verifier's verification and "the Commission and the competent authority to review the CBAM declaration" — the verifier's output is a structural input to R's oversight); **Evidence YES** (Articles 6, 7, 8, Annex VI are all operative; Recitals 25 and 47 are interpretive support only); **Counterfactual YES** (removing verification would leave the declaration an unverified self-report, undermining the entire review/certificate-surrender architecture). High confidence.

**rel-004 — Independent certification of carbon-price documentation (Art 9).** The declarant (T) may claim a reduction in certificates to be surrendered based on carbon price already paid abroad; Article 9(2) requires the supporting documentation to be certified by a person independent of both the declarant and the country-of-origin authorities (I). Third-actor test: all five dimensions **YES** on the same logic as rel-003 (distinct, specific function, directly feeds a quantity that affects the declarant's certificate-surrender obligation reviewed by R, anchored in Art 9(1)-(2), and its removal would leave the reduction claim without any assurance mechanism). Medium confidence because the qualifications/independence criteria for this person are left to a future implementing act (Art 9(4)), even though the current operative anchor for the relation itself is solid.

**rel-006 — Verification of embedded emissions, operator channel (Art 10(5)-(7), Annex VI).** A distinct, upstream node from rel-003: an operator of a third-country installation (T) who registers in the CBAM registry (Commission, R) must have its own embedded-emissions calculation verified by an accredited verifier (I) before that data can be disclosed to, and relied on by, an authorised CBAM declarant. Third-actor test: **Distinction/Function/Evidence YES** on the same basis as rel-003; **Regulatory integration YES** because Article 10(6) explicitly links the operator's records to both verification and "the review, in accordance with Article 19, of the CBAM declaration made by an authorised CBAM declarant to whom the relevant information was disclosed"; **Counterfactual YES** because unverified operator data could not be relied upon within the declarant's own verification obligation (Art 8(2)), breaking a step of the upstream data-quality chain. High confidence. Kept separate from rel-003 because T differs materially (third-country operator vs. authorised CBAM declarant) even though the same class of intermediary and mechanism recur.

**rel-008 — Customs authorities enforce the authorised-declarant-only import rule (Art 4, 25(1)).** Article 4 establishes that only an authorised CBAM declarant may import covered goods; the competent authority (R) is the body that grants or withholds that status (Art 17). Article 25(1) has customs authorities (I) refuse importation at the border to any person who is not an authorised CBAM declarant (T, here the would-be importer). Third-actor test: **Distinction YES** (customs administrations, Art 3(14), are institutionally distinct from the CBAM competent authority, Art 3(13)); **Function YES** (Art 25(1) attributes a specific gatekeeping act); **Regulatory integration YES** (customs authorities do not decide authorisation status themselves — they enforce a determination made by the competent authority — a textbook enforcement-support function); **Evidence YES** (Art 4 and 25(1) are operative; Recital 43 is interpretive support); **Counterfactual YES** (without the border-control function, Article 4's requirement would have no operative enforcement mechanism). High confidence.

**rel-009 — Customs data transmission enabling oversight, definitive period (Art 13(2), 15(2), 19(3), 25(2)-(4)).** Customs authorities (I) periodically and automatically transmit import data to the Commission (R), which cross-checks it against the CBAM registry (Art 25(3)) and folds it into risk-based review of declarants (Art 19(3)); Article 13(2) further permits sharing confidential customs information for compliance purposes. Third-actor test: all five dimensions **YES** — distinct institutional role, specific and repeated data-communication function, directly built into the Commission's cross-check and risk-analysis mechanisms toward the declarant (T), anchored throughout in operative text, and its removal would strip the Commission's oversight of a primary independent data source. High confidence.

**rel-010 — Customs communication during the transitional period (Art 33(1)-(3)).** Structurally identical to rel-009 but for the lighter, certificate-free transitional-period reporting regime: customs authorities (I) both inform importers/indirect customs representatives (T) of the Article 35 reporting obligation and transmit import data to the Commission (R), which relays it to competent authorities. Third-actor test: all five dimensions **YES** on the same reasoning as rel-009. High confidence.

Across these seven intermediated relations, the recurring intermediary types are: a narrow certifying actor embedded in an eligibility/reduction-claim decision (tax authority, rel-002; independent person, rel-004), an accredited verifier performing the central assurance function of the whole CBAM compliance architecture (rel-003, rel-006), and customs authorities performing both enforcement-support and information-transmission functions at the border (rel-008, rel-009, rel-010). No intermediary was found to be merely mentioned, consulted, or a passive information recipient — each satisfies the regulatory-integration and counterfactual dimensions on the text's own terms.

## D. Important direct relations

- **rel-001 (Authorisation, Art 4/5/17):** the foundational licensing relationship — competent authority grants, refuses or revokes "authorised CBAM declarant" status. T can be occupied by the importer itself or, where applicable, its indirect customs representative (a role-substitution, not a mediating third actor).
- **rel-005 (Registration of third-country operators, Art 10(1)-(4),(8)):** a direct Commission-operator administrative relationship (registration, updates, deregistration), separate from the verification relation (rel-006) that sits alongside it.
- **rel-007 (Accreditation of verifiers, Art 18(1)-(2)):** the national accreditation body accredits verifiers based on its own documented assessment criterion; this credentialing relation underlies both verification relations (rel-003, rel-006) but is analytically upstream and distinct from them.
- **rel-011 (CBAM certificate lifecycle, Art 20-24, 31):** the sale/price/surrender/repurchase/cancellation cycle between the Member State/Commission and the declarant; the common central platform and CBAM registry are treated as instruments, not actors.
- **rel-012 (Review & risk-analysis oversight, Art 15, 19, 22(3)):** Commission and competent authority coded as co-regulators (both hold independent review powers) rather than R/I, with a specific tension around the Commission's Art 19(4) preliminary calculation documented in the uncertainty log (U2) rather than forced into a separate relation.
- **rel-013 (Penalties, Art 26):** direct enforcement by the competent authority against declarants and non-compliant importers.
- **rel-014 (Transitional reporting, Art 32/35):** the certificate-free precursor to the definitive-period declaration cycle.
- **rel-015 (Third-country electricity exemption, Art 2(7)-(9)):** flagged `[reviewer_attention: conceptual_boundary]` because a third country/territory as T sits at the edge of how RIT is usually applied within an EU-internal regulator/regulatee frame; no mediating third actor is attributed a function here (the third country itself submits its roadmap and reports directly).

## E. Borderline cases

See `uncertainties` in the JSON for full detail; summarised here:

1. **U1 — Accreditation body / Commission (Art 18(3)).** Considered whether the Commission's prospective delegated-act power over accreditation conditions, oversight and withdrawal recasts rel-007 as intermediated (Commission=R, accreditation body=I, verifier=T). Not coded positive: Article 18(2) currently gives the accreditation body its own decisional criterion, and Article 18(3)'s power is not yet substantively exercised in this corpus.
2. **U2 — Commission's preliminary calculation within rel-012 (Art 19(4)-(5), 26(4)).** A genuine, unresolved tension between reading the Commission as a co-regulator (as coded) and as a distinct intermediary feeding the competent authority's determination — closely analogous to the unresolved Case 01 Article 8(4)/EEA tension flagged in the working draft. Left open rather than forced either way.
3. **U3 — Financial institution issuing the Art 17(5) bank guarantee.** Considered and rejected: a private commercial instrument, not a regulatory function integrated into the competent authority's supervision of the declarant.
4. **U4 — Peer competent authorities/Commission consulted before granting or revoking authorisation (Art 17(1), (8)).** Considered and rejected: co-regulator consultation supporting the deciding authority's own internal process, not a function directed at the applicant/declarant.
5. **U5 — EPPO and "authorities in charge of ... penalties" (Art 13(2)).** Considered and rejected: named only as permitted recipients of shared information, with no attributed function in this corpus.
6. **U6 — Circumvention notifiers, including NGOs (Art 27(3)-(5)).** Genuinely borderline on two compounding grounds: Article 27's operative text does not clearly individuate an actor-class T (it addresses practices/goods/Annex amendments rather than a named target), and the Commission's own-motion investigation power weakens the counterfactual necessity of the notification channel. Logged rather than coded either as intermediated or forced into direct.
7. **U7 — Climate Club (Recital 72), `contextual_candidate_recital_only`.** Coordination/monitoring-quality functions described only in the preamble; no operative article establishes or empowers it.
8. **U8 — Union financial/technical assistance to LDCs (Recitals 71/73/74; Art 30(8)), `contextual_candidate_recital_only`.** The only operative counterpart is a Commission reporting duty about an external financing instrument, not an assistance relationship of this Regulation's own.
9. **U9 — Commission's Art 12 coordination role and Art 11(2) inter-authority exchange.** Considered and rejected: coordination among co-regulators about their own functioning, not a function toward an identifiable regulated target.

## F. Primary-coder caveats

- **Third-country/international provisions at the model's edge.** Two relations (rel-015; the rejected candidate in U6) push at the boundary of how the RIT model is typically applied to intra-Union regulator/regulatee relationships. They are flagged for reviewer attention rather than silently normalised into an ordinary domestic-style R-T relation or excluded outright.
- **Prospective delegated/implementing acts.** Several relations (rel-002's independent-person qualifications under Art 9(4); rel-007's Art 18(3) accreditation standards; numerous Art 7(7)/Art 29(2) implementing-act cross-references throughout Annex IV) depend for their fine-grained detail on delegated or implementing acts not yet adopted within this frozen corpus. Where the R-I-T structure and mechanism were nonetheless clearly supported by the base Regulation's own operative text, the relation was coded; where the structure itself depended on not-yet-existing standards (U1), it was left in the uncertainty log.
- **External cross-references not imported.** Exact figures and procedural detail governed by Regulation (EU) No 952/2013 (Union Customs Code), Directive 2003/87/EC (EU ETS, including the Article 26 penalty quantum), Regulation (EC) No 765/2008, and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/2067 were not imported; where a relation's core structure did not depend on that external detail, it was coded and the limitation noted in `coder_note`; nothing was filled in by assumption.
- **No target count was pursued.** The relation set reflects what the operative text, read in full and re-checked in a second pass focused on verification/reporting/certification/accreditation/delegated-implementation/information-flow/supervisory patterns, actually supports — not an attempt to match or diverge from any prior count.
