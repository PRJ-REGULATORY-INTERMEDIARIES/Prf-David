## HQ-DIR-01

**Case:** Council Directive 86/361/EEC — initial stage of mutual recognition of type approval for telecommunications terminal equipment  
**Comparative level:** C3  
**Issue type(s):** H5 (existence, number or boundary of architecture); H8 (external legal dependency); H9 (possible over-inference); H10 (material proposition present in only one arm); H11 (distinct stopping levels with substantive consequence)  
**Priority:** HIGH  
**Depends on evidence outside the Regulation's text:** YES

**Common legal evidence:** Council Directive 86/361/EEC: Art. 4(2) (Commission requests the CEPT to draw up specifications in the form of NETs); Art. 6(1)–(3) (NETs regarded as equivalent of common conformity specifications; no further tests where a certificate of conformity with a published specification exists; recognition for type approval; specifications used by competent authorities); Art. 7(3); Art. 8(1)–(5) (suspension of recognition; consultation; laboratory opinion; Committee referral; withdrawal from the published list; revocation); Art. 2(8), (15) (approved testing laboratory; NET).

**Proposition A:** The same recognition–specification–laboratory–suspension coupling is stated, but the architectural level is not asserted because the constitution of NETs, of approved laboratories and of the safeguard branches rests on instruments outside the Directive; the arrangement stops at the subconfiguration level, and alternative groupings (recognition regime with specification as external input; stand-alone safeguard; several independent architectures) are stated.

**Proposition B:** The Directive constitutes a bounded formal legal architecture for conformity assurance and mutual recognition of the covered process, with high confidence: coordinated specification work, approved testing and certification, certificate-based recognition removing repeat tests, national type approval, and a Commission-level safeguard.

**Exact disagreement:** Whether the architectural level can be asserted for the covered process from the Directive alone, with what confidence, and whether the rival groupings change the claim.

**Question:** Do the cited provisions support asserting an architecture for the covered process (and with what confidence), or only the subconfiguration-level couplings, given the external instruments on which NETs and approved laboratories rest?
