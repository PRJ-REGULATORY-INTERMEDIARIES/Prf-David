# Architecture register — V8 C2 CBAM

## A1 — Formal CBAM governance architecture

- **Bearer/property:** the configuration of formal governance arrangements established or recognized by Regulation (EU) 2023/956, rather than the statute as an undifferentiated object, any single actor, or any single provision.
- **Status:** higher-order inference; formal legal design only.
- **Evidence base:** configurations CFG1–CFG5 and claims C02–C15, grounded in Articles 4–30 and 32–35, especially the linked authorization/declaration, verification, registry, certificate, review and enforcement provisions.
- **Inferential step:** the cited provisions establish functionally connected and cross-referenced arrangements: authorization conditions importation; declarants report emissions; operators/verifiers produce and assure emissions information; the Commission and national authorities use the registry and customs data to administer, review and enforce; certificates translate declared emissions into purchase/holding/surrender duties with consequences for non-compliance. Their conjunction forms a patterned structure distributing governing work, authority, discretion and answerability across identified positions. This is configurational significance, not a count of provisions or actors.
- **Confidence:** medium-high for the existence and broad organization of the formal architecture; lower for relative centrality or the practical importance of any component.
- **Limit:** no implementation, operational effectiveness, external legal corpus, or complete regime claim follows.

## Not inferred

No categorical architecture type, dominant governance logic, strategy classification, regime boundary, or architecture-among-regimes claim is registered. Article 1 states CBAM complements the EU ETS, which warrants recording that express legal connection, but this source does not reconstruct either scheme's full architecture or their regime-level relation. The formal CBAM architecture can be described without deciding those broader questions.
