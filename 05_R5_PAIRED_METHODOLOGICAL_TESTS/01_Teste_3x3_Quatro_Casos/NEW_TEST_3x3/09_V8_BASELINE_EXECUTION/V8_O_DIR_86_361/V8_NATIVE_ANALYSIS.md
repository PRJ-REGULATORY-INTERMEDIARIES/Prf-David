# V8 native analysis — Directive 86/361/EEC

## Result

The Directive establishes a formal, multilevel conformity-assurance and mutual-recognition arrangement for mass-produced telecommunications terminal equipment. The most consequential configuration combines technical specification work through CEPT (with Commission coordination and committee consultation), testing by approved laboratories, certificate-based mutual recognition, and type approval by competent Member State authorities. The recognition rule removes repeat testing for covered results while preserving national approval authority and a reasoned safeguard route. This combination warrants a bounded formal legal architecture inference for the Directive's subject matter. It does not warrant claims about implementation, practical centrality, effectiveness, a complete telecommunications regulatory regime, or a governance logic for the wider sector.

## Evidence-sensitive limits

“Mutual recognition” is not equated with automatic type approval: Article 6(2) says certificates are recognized for type-approval purposes and bars further tests in defined conditions, while approval remains with competent authorities. Committee consultation is not recoded as binding committee authority. Laboratory accreditation/approval is not attributed to a single actor where Article 2(8) names both the appropriate Member State and a body recognized as competent. Procurement waiver conditions are kept distinct from approval/testing safeguards. No operational, effectiveness, or frequency-based inference is made.
