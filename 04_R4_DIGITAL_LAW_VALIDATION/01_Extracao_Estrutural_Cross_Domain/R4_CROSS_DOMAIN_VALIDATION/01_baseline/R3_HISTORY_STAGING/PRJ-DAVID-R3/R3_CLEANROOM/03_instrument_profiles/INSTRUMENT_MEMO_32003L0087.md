# 1. Executive summary

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1-3, 4-21, 24-31; Annexes I-V]** This Directive establishes a Union greenhouse-gas emission allowance trading scheme. Its legal system combines coverage rules, installation permits, allocation of allowances, monitoring and reporting, verification, surrender and cancellation, registries, penalties, public access and controlled expansion or linkage.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-20; Annexes I-V]** The instrument operates as a compliance market: covered installations must be authorized and produce verified emissions information; allowances are allocated and transferable; operators must surrender allowances matching verified emissions; registries and sanctions support enforceability.

**[ANALYTICAL_INFERENCE]** The declared orientation is a compliance-oriented market-organization regime because the trading market is legally organized around an installation-level permit and surrender obligation rather than constituted as a free-standing market.

# 2. Declared regulatory objective

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 1]** The declared objective is to establish a greenhouse-gas emission allowance trading scheme in order to promote reductions in a cost-effective and economically efficient manner.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Recital 5]** The Directive also expressly connects the scheme to the Community and Member States' emission-reduction commitments and to limiting adverse effects on economic development and employment.

# 3. Regulatory object

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1-3; Annexes I-II]** The primary regulatory object is greenhouse-gas emissions from specified activities and gases, together with the allowance system used to account for and trade permission to emit.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-16, 19-20; Annexes IV-V]** The object includes the information and transaction infrastructure through which emissions become legally actionable: permits, measurement or calculation, reports, verification, allowance accounts and surrender.

# 4. Principal target classes

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 2-7, 12, 14-16; Annex I]** The principal governed class is operators of installations carrying out Annex I activities above the relevant thresholds, including new entrants and installations subject to temporary exclusion rules.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 9-13, 17, 19]** Other broad governed classes are Member States in their allocation and registry arrangements, and persons who hold or transfer allowances. The Directive also creates public-access and information constituencies without treating them as relation-specific roles.

# 5. Principal regulatory authorities

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 4-8, 11, 14-18, 20-23, 27-31]** Member States and designated competent authorities administer permits, allocation, monitoring, penalties and national registries. The Commission develops guidance, adopts monitoring/reporting and registry rules, reviews national allocation plans, maintains the central transaction log through a designated Central Administrator, and coordinates implementation through the relevant committee.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 15 and Annex V]** Independent verifiers are a formal verification component. Their presence is recorded at instrument level as an authority/responsibility structure, without assigning them an R/I/T role.

# 6. Legal scope

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 2; Annexes I-II]** Material scope covers emissions from activities listed in Annex I and greenhouse gases listed in Annex II, subject to the thresholds and exclusions in Annex I. Research, development and testing installations are excluded in Annex I.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 12, 24-27]** The territorial core is the Community scheme administered by Member States; transfers may involve third-country persons where recognized, and linkage with third-country schemes is contemplated. Member States may seek unilateral inclusion or temporary exclusion under controlled conditions.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 4, 11, 13, 27, 32]** The operative system begins for installations from 1 January 2005, is organized through trading periods, permits and annual surrender, and includes a temporary exclusion route and a continuing review/expansion architecture.

# 7. Major obligation clusters

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-8]** Permit and coverage control: covered installations require a greenhouse-gas emissions permit; applications and permit contents must identify activities, sources, monitoring and reporting; changes to installations must be notified.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 9-13]** Allocation and market operation: Member States prepare allocation plans, allocate and issue allowances, recognize transfers, and ensure surrender and cancellation against verified emissions.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 14-15; Annexes IV-V]** Information and assurance: operators monitor and report emissions; reports are verified against methodological, reliability and completeness criteria.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 16-21]** Compliance infrastructure: penalties, publication of non-compliant operators, competent authorities, registries, automated transaction checks and Member State reporting support enforcement and oversight.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 24-30]** Controlled extension, linkage, temporary exclusion, pooling and periodic review allow the scheme to adapt without abandoning monitoring, reporting and market-integrity conditions.

# 8. Main governance components

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-23; Annexes III-V]** The main components are: coverage and permit authorization; allocation planning; emissions information production; independent verification; allowance accounting and registry control; compliance and sanctions; public information; Commission review and delegated/committee implementation.

# 9. Structural logic

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-20; Annexes I-V]** Covered activity and gas -> installation permit -> monitoring and reporting -> verification -> allocation and allowance holding -> surrender/cancellation -> registry and transaction checks -> penalties, public access and review.

# 10. Declared regulatory orientation

**[ANALYTICAL_INFERENCE — Articles 1, 4-20 and Annexes I-V]** Compliance-oriented market-organization regime. The market is not the sole endpoint: the instrument makes verified emissions and mandatory surrender the organizing conditions of allowance trading.

# 12. Analytical uncertainties

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT / ANALYTICAL_UNCERTAINTY — Articles 25-26, 30]** The Directive expressly anticipates links, amendments and future expansion, but this Phase 3 profile uses only the original act as adopted and does not reconstruct later consolidated operation.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION / ANALYTICAL_UNCERTAINTY — Articles 8, 18-20]** Some administrative functions are distributed between Member States, competent authorities, registries and the Central Administrator; the profile records them as institutional components without deciding relation-specific roles.
