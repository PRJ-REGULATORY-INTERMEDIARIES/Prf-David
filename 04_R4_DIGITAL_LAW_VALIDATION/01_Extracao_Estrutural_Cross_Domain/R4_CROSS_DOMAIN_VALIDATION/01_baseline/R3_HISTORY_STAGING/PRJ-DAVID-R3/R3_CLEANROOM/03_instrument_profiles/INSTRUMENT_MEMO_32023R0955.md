# 1. Executive summary

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1 and 3]** This Regulation establishes the Social Climate Fund for 2026-2032. It finances Member State Social Climate Plans addressing the social effects of including building and road-transport emissions within the EU ETS scope.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-27; Annexes I-V]** The instrument links vulnerability assessment and public consultation to Plans, eligible measures, financial allocations, milestones, conditional payments, control systems, public information, monitoring and evaluation.

**[ANALYTICAL_INFERENCE]** The declared orientation is a conditional social-financing and implementation-governance instrument.

# 2. Declared regulatory objective

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 3]** The general objective is to contribute to a socially fair transition toward climate neutrality by addressing social impacts from the inclusion of building and road-transport emissions in Directive 2003/87/EC.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 3(2)]** Specific objectives are support for vulnerable households, vulnerable micro-enterprises and vulnerable transport users through temporary income support and measures for building efficiency, heating/cooling decarbonisation, renewable energy and low-emission mobility.

# 3. Regulatory object

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1-3]** The regulatory object is the social impact of carbon pricing in buildings and road transport and the Union financial system used to fund responses through Member State Plans.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-21 and Annexes III-V]** The object includes the Plan lifecycle, allocation of resources, milestones and targets, payment conditions, data systems, audit, fraud controls and evaluation.

# 4. Principal target classes

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1-9]** The principal broad governed/beneficiary classes are vulnerable households, vulnerable micro-enterprises and vulnerable transport users, including energy-poor or transport-poor groups as defined in Article 2.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 4-8, 21]** Member States are governed as Plan submitters and implementers. Public and private entities may carry out measures that ultimately benefit the defined vulnerable classes, but Phase 3 records them only as broad implementation classes.

# 5. Principal regulatory authorities

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 4-6, 12, 16-27]** The Commission assesses and approves Plans, determines or implements financial decisions, authorizes payments, monitors implementation and conducts evaluation and review. Member States prepare, consult on, implement, monitor and control Plans.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 21, 23-26 and Annex III]** The European Parliament and Council receive information and participate in transparency/dialogue; the Commission, OLAF, Court of Auditors and, where applicable, EPPO have audit/control access; local and regional authorities and stakeholders enter through consultation and Plan governance.

# 6. Legal scope

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 1]** Material scope is the Fund's financial support for measures and investments in Plans that address the effects of building and road-transport emissions pricing, especially energy poverty and transport poverty.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1, 10, 29]** The Fund period is 2026-2032, with application from 30 June 2024; payment completion is set for 31 December 2033 and the Regulation is directly applicable in Member States. The act also contains Plan milestones through 31 July 2032 and an ex post evaluation by 31 December 2033.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 7-9]** Eligible scope includes building renovation, energy and heating/cooling decarbonisation, renewable generation/storage, sustainable mobility and limited temporary direct income support; measures must observe “do no significant harm” and pass benefits to the vulnerable classes.

# 7. Major obligation clusters

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-6]** Plan design and participation: Member States submit coherent Plans, consult specified publics, identify effects and vulnerable classes, define measures, financing, milestones, indicators and controls, and use the prescribed template.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 7-15]** Support and finance: the Fund supports eligible measures, imposes climate and environmental conditions, sets resource and allocation rules, requires additionality and a minimum national contribution, and permits specified transfers and technical assistance.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 16-20]** Assessment and conditional disbursement: the Commission tests relevance, effectiveness, efficiency and coherence, decides on Plans, commits allocations, and pays against satisfactorily achieved milestones and targets, with suspension, reduction and termination rules.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 21-26; Annexes III-IV]** Financial integrity, transparency and monitoring: internal controls, anti-fraud measures, audit data, public information, biennial reports, common indicators and institutional dialogue support accountability.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 27]** Evaluation and review: implementation, efficiency, poverty definitions, continued relevance and Fund impact are reviewed, including an independent ex post evaluation.

# 8. Main governance components

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-27]** The components are: vulnerability and impact assessment; participatory Plan design; eligible social/climate investment; direct Union financial management; Commission appraisal; milestone-based disbursement; internal control and audit; standardized data and transparency; monitoring and evaluation; coordination with other Union instruments.

# 9. Structural logic

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-21]** carbon-price impact and vulnerability assessment -> consulted Social Climate Plan -> Commission relevance/effectiveness/efficiency/coherence assessment -> approved measures and milestones -> payment on satisfactory achievement -> monitoring, audit, transparency, suspension/recovery and evaluation.

# 10. Declared regulatory orientation

**[ANALYTICAL_INFERENCE — Articles 1, 3-8, 16-21]** Conditional social-financing and implementation-governance instrument. Financial support is legally tied to Plans, environmental and social conditions, milestones, data, controls and payment decisions.

# 12. Analytical uncertainties

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT / ANALYTICAL_UNCERTAINTY — Articles 1, 10-11 and 29]** Fund resources and timing are linked in part to Directive 2003/87/EC provisions outside this act; the profile records the dependency but does not reconstruct the external financing source.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT / ANALYTICAL_UNCERTAINTY — Article 9]** Support may pass through public or private entities, but the instrument-level profile does not decide whether any such entity is an intermediary in a specific relation.
