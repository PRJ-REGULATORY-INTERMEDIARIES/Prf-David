# 1. Executive summary

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1 and 4]** This Regulation imposes binding Member State greenhouse-gas reduction obligations for 2021-2030 in covered non-ETS sectors and sets a 2030 limit for each Member State.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-12]** It converts national data into annual allocations and a linear trajectory, allows bounded flexibility, provides corrective and compliance consequences, and maintains a Union Registry and review system.

**[ANALYTICAL_INFERENCE]** The declared orientation is a binding national target-setting and compliance framework.

# 2. Declared regulatory objective

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 1]** The Regulation lays down Member State obligations concerning minimum contributions from 2021 to 2030 toward a 30% reduction below 2005 levels in the sectors covered by the Regulation, and contributes to the Paris Agreement objectives.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 1]** It also expressly establishes rules for annual emission allocations and evaluation of Member State progress.

# 3. Regulatory object

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 2-4; Annex I]** The regulatory object is Member State greenhouse-gas emissions in specified non-ETS categories and the annual emission allocations that define their permitted trajectory.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-12; Annexes II-IV]** The object includes the accounting of flexibilities, removals, allowances, corrective action and registry transactions used to determine compliance.

# 4. Principal target classes

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 1, 4-11]** The principal directly governed class is Member States, whose annual and 2030 emissions, intended flexibilities, corrective plans and compliance are regulated.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 4, 8, 12-16]** National inventory and policy-reporting systems, the Union Registry and the public-access information system are broad institutional objects of governance. This phase does not transform them into relation-specific actor roles.

# 5. Principal regulatory authorities

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 4(3), 8, 10, 12-15]** The Commission adopts implementing acts setting annual allocations, reviews national data, assesses progress, publishes adjustments, adopts delegated acts for registry accounting and conducts reviews.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 8-9, 12, 14]** The European Environment Agency assists assessment; the Central Administrator operates the Registry compliance functions; and the Climate Change Committee assists the Commission's committee procedure.

# 6. Legal scope

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Article 2]** Material scope covers IPCC energy, industrial-process/product-use, agriculture and waste categories, excluding emissions from Annex I activities under Directive 2003/87/EC. LULUCF emissions and removals are generally excluded except for Article 7 and Article 9(2) interactions; civil-aviation CO2 in the specified category is treated as zero.

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT — Articles 4-11; Annexes I-IV]** Territorial scope is Union-wide and Member-State specific. Temporal scope is 2021-2030, with 2030 limits, annual allocations, 2027 and 2032 compliance checks, and review linked to reporting and Paris Agreement cycles.

# 7. Major obligation clusters

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 1, 4 and Annex I]** Target and allocation setting: Member States must meet 2030 limits and annual linear-trajectory limits; the Commission reviews inventory data and fixes annual allocations.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 5-7 and Annexes II-III]** Controlled flexibility: borrowing, banking and transfers, limited EU ETS allowance cancellation, and bounded use of net removals may count toward compliance under conditions.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 8-11]** Corrective and compliance control: insufficient progress triggers a corrective action plan; excess emissions trigger additions and transfer restrictions; a conditional safety reserve may address specified shortfalls.

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 12-16]** Accounting, rulemaking and review: registry accounting, automated transaction checks, public access, delegated and committee procedures, reporting amendments and periodic review maintain the system.

# 8. Main governance components

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-16]** The components are: national target setting; inventory review; annual allocation; bounded flexibility; corrective action; compliance checking; Registry accounting; Commission and EEA assessment; delegated/implementing rulemaking; reporting and review.

# 9. Structural logic

**[CROSS_PROVISION_RECONSTRUCTION — Articles 4-12]** national inventory data -> Commission-reviewed 2030 limits and annual allocations -> permitted flexibilities -> annual progress and corrective action -> 2027/2032 compliance check -> registry accounting, restrictions and review.

# 10. Declared regulatory orientation

**[ANALYTICAL_INFERENCE — Articles 1, 4-12]** Binding national target-setting and compliance framework. The core is a legally quantified national trajectory with conditional flexibility and consequences for non-compliance.

# 12. Analytical uncertainties

**[EXPLICIT_TEXT / ANALYTICAL_UNCERTAINTY — Articles 2, 5-7, 12 and 16]** The Regulation depends on Regulations 2018/841, 2018/1999 and Directive 2003/87/EC for data, removals, reporting and allowances. Only the provisions present in this validated corpus are reconstructed.

**[ANALYTICAL_UNCERTAINTY]** Transfers and market intermediaries are mentioned in Article 5(7), but Phase 3 records them only as part of the legal flexibility architecture and does not code intermediary status or relations.
