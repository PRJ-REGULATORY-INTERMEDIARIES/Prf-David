01 / Why this comparison matters

Different units. Shared theory.

Both projects draw on regulatory intermediation and both take the legal text seriously. Their differences concern what is recorded as an observation and the kinds of claims the evidence is designed to support.

05 / Evidence and scope

What is supported—and what is not.

The comparison is grounded in the working codebook, Rotem's correspondence, and the V8 four-case summaries. Status labels distinguish direct overlap from future comparison.

Scientific boundary: a claim of dataset-level agreement, increased validity or greater explanatory performance would require matching the underlying coded records. This site illustrates research designs and identifies the most informative comparison—not a completed validation result.
06 / Discussion proposition

Complementarity as a testable claim.

Rather than merging methods or duplicating coding, begin with the shared historical case and ask what each representation makes observable.

Suggested framing for David: Rotem's design maps who intermediates, how, and how that changes over time. The V8/RGAM exercise examines how legal relationships are assembled into defensible architectural claims. Together, the approaches may provide institutional depth and inferential transparency—subject to a focused comparison of their actual records.
